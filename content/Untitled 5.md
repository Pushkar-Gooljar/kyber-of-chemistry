# 👁️ Visual Profiling: Deducing Reagents by Appearance
**Tags:** #chemistry #alevel #paper3 #visual-cues #reagents

In QA, your eyes are your first analytical tool. Many transition metals and specific reagents have highly distinct appearances. If you are handed an unknown "FA" solution or solid, use this matrix to immediately narrow down its identity before doing a single test.

## 💧 1. Aqueous Solutions (Liquids)

| Visual Appearance             | Highly Likely Identity                               | Context / Exam Strategy                                                                                                                                                        |
| :---------------------------- | :--------------------------------------------------- | :----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Deep Purple**               | **Potassium Manganate(VII)** ($KMnO_4$)              | It is a powerful oxidising agent. Expect to add it to a reducing agent (like $Fe^{2+}$, $H_2O_2$, or an alcohol) and watch it turn **colourless** (or pale pink).              |
| **Bright Orange**             | **Potassium Dichromate(VI)** ($K_2Cr_2O_7$)          | Another oxidising agent. Expect it to turn **green** when it reacts with a reducing agent (like a primary/secondary alcohol or $SO_2$).                                        |
| **Pale Blue**                 | **Copper(II) ions** ($Cu^{2+}$)                      | Immediately anticipate the blue ppt with $NaOH$, and the distinct **pale blue ppt dissolving to a deep/dark blue solution** with excess $NH_3$.                                |
| **Pale Green**                | **Iron(II) ions** ($Fe^{2+}$)                        | Anticipate a green ppt with $NaOH/NH_3$ that slowly turns **brown** at the surface as it oxidises in the air.                                                                  |
| **Yellow / Brown / Rust**     | **Iron(III) ions** ($Fe^{3+}$) or **Iodine** ($I_2$) | To distinguish: add $NaOH$. $Fe^{3+}$ gives a red-brown ppt. $I_2$ will decolourise or form a haloform if heated with a methyl ketone. (Adding starch turns $I_2$ blue-black). |
| **Yellow / Orange (organic)** | **2,4-DNPH** (Brady's Reagent)                       | Used exclusively to test for aldehydes and ketones. Gives an orange/yellow precipitate.                                                                                        |

## 🪨 2. Solid Powders & Crystals

| Visual Appearance | Highly Likely Identity | Expected Thermal Behaviour |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Green Powder** | **Copper(II) Carbonate** ($CuCO_3$) | Heating will turn it into a **black solid** ($CuO$) and give off $CO_2$ (test with limewater). |
| **Blue Crystals** | Hydrated **Copper(II) salt** (e.g., $CuSO_4 \cdot 5H_2O$) | Heating will produce **condensation** on the upper tube, and the blue crystals will turn into a **white powder** (anhydrous salt). |
| **Black Powder** | **Manganese(IV) Oxide** ($MnO_2$) or **Copper(II) Oxide** ($CuO$) | If $MnO_2$, it acts as a catalyst. Adding $H_2O_2$ will cause violent fizzing ($O_2$ evolved) but the black solid will remain unchanged. |
| **Dark Purple / Black Crystals** | Solid **$KMnO_4$** | Heating strongly will cause it to "jump" or crackle (decrepitation) and release oxygen gas. |
| **Pale Green Crystals** | Hydrated **Iron(II) salt** | Will produce condensation on heating and turn into a brown/rust solid as it oxidises. |
| **Rust / Orange-Brown Powder** | **Iron(III) salt** | Dissolving in water yields a yellow-brown solution. |
| **White Powder / Crystals** | Group 1, Group 2, Ammonium, Zinc, or Aluminium salts | Will not be a transition metal. You must rely purely on chemical tests (e.g., Flame tests, $NaOH/NH_3$ solubility, thermal decomposition). |

## ⚙️ 3. Metals (Elements)

If you are handed a solid element to use as a reagent or test subject, its physical appearance gives it away immediately.

| Visual Appearance | Likely Identity | Typical Exam Use |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Silver / Grey Ribbon** | **Magnesium** ($Mg$) | Added to acids to prove the presence of $H^+$ (effervescence, $H_2$ gas pops). Also used to test reactivity/displacement. |
| **Silver / Grey Foil** | **Aluminium** ($Al$) | Used specifically in **Devarda's test**. Added to $NaOH$ and heated to test for Nitrates ($NO_3^-$) or Nitrites ($NO_2^-$). Generates $NH_3$ gas. |
| **Grey Powder** | **Zinc** ($Zn$) | Often used as a reducing agent in redox reactions or displacement. Turns solutions of $Cu^{2+}$ from blue to colourless while a brown solid forms. |
| **Red-Brown / Pink-Brown Solid** | **Copper** ($Cu$) | Usually not given as a starting reagent, but is **formed** as a precipitate during a displacement reaction (e.g., when adding $Zn$ or $Mg$ to a $Cu^{2+}$ solution). |

> [!tip] The "Identify the Metal" Hack
> If the exam asks you to "Identify the metal M" and it's a silver/grey ribbon that fizzes rapidly with acid, **it is Magnesium**. If it's foil and they ask you to add it to NaOH, **it is Aluminium**. The exam board rarely deviates from this physical prep.
