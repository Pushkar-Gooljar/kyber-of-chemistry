# 17. Carbonyl Compounds (Aldehydes and Ketones)

**Tags:** `#A-Level-Chemistry` `#9701` `#Organic-Chemistry` `#Carbonyls`

## 1. General Structure and Physical Properties
Carbonyl compounds contain the carbon-oxygen double bond ($C=O$). 
*   **Aldehydes** have the carbonyl group at the *end* of the carbon chain (suffix **-al**). General formula: $RCHO$.
*   **Ketones** have the carbonyl group *within* the carbon chain (suffix **-one**). General formula: $RCOR'$.

> [!abstract] Diagram: Structure of the Carbonyl Group
> *   **Hybridisation:** The carbonyl carbon is **$sp^2$ hybridised**.
> *   **Shape:** **Trigonal planar** around the carbonyl carbon with a bond angle of **$120^\circ$**.
> *   **Bonding:** The $C=O$ double bond consists of one $\sigma$ (sigma) bond and one $\pi$ (pi) bond (formed by sideways overlap of p-orbitals).
> *   **Polarity:** Oxygen is much more electronegative than carbon, drawing electron density towards itself. This creates a permanent dipole: $C^{\delta+} = O^{\delta-}$.

### Physical Properties
*   **Boiling Points:** Higher than alkanes of similar relative molecular mass due to **permanent dipole-dipole interactions**. Lower than alcohols because carbonyls **cannot form hydrogen bonds with each other** (no $O-H$ bond).
*   **Solubility in Water:** Lower members (methanal, ethanal, propanone) are highly soluble. The lone pairs on the carbonyl oxygen can form **hydrogen bonds with water molecules**. Solubility decreases as the non-polar hydrocarbon chain gets longer.

## 2. Preparation of Carbonyl Compounds
Aldehydes and ketones are produced via the oxidation of alcohols. 
**Reagents:** Acidified Potassium Dichromate(VI) ($K_2Cr_2O_7 / H^+$) OR Acidified Potassium Manganate(VII) ($KMnO_4 / H^+$).

### 2.1 Formation of Aldehydes
*   **Reactant:** Primary ($1^\circ$) alcohol.
*   **Conditions:** Heat gently and **distil off the product immediately** as it forms.
*   **Why distil?** To prevent the aldehyde from remaining in the oxidizing mixture and being further oxidized to a carboxylic acid.
*   **Equation:** $RCH_2OH + [O] \rightarrow RCHO + H_2O$
*   **Observation:** Orange $Cr_2O_7^{2-}$ turns to **green** $Cr^{3+}$ (or Purple $MnO_4^-$ turns **colorless** $Mn^{2+}$).

### 2.2 Formation of Ketones
*   **Reactant:** Secondary ($2^\circ$) alcohol.
*   **Conditions:** Heat under **reflux**. 
*   **Note:** Ketones cannot be easily further oxidized, so reflux ensures the reaction goes to completion without risking over-oxidation.
*   **Equation:** $RCH(OH)R' + [O] \rightarrow RCOR' + H_2O$

---

## 3. Reduction Reactions
Aldehydes and ketones can be reduced back to alcohols. Both reagents provide a source of the nucleophilic hydride ion ($H^-$), which attacks the $C^{\delta+}$.

> [!warning] Reagents and Conditions (Highly Tested)
> Examiners heavily test the distinction in conditions between the two reducing agents:
> 1.  **Sodium borohydride ($NaBH_4$):** Dissolved in **water or alkaline aqueous methanol**. Can be done at room temperature or warmed. (Weaker reducing agent).
> 2.  **Lithium aluminium hydride ($LiAlH_4$):** Must be used in **dry ether** at room temperature. (Stronger reducing agent; reacts violently with water).

*   **Aldehydes $\rightarrow$ Primary Alcohols:** $RCHO + 2[H] \rightarrow RCH_2OH$
*   **Ketones $\rightarrow$ Secondary Alcohols:** $RCOR' + 2[H] \rightarrow RCH(OH)R'$

## 4. Nucleophilic Addition of HCN
The electron-deficient $C^{\delta+}$ atom in the polar $C=O$ bond is highly susceptible to attack by nucleophiles. The most important of these is the addition of hydrogen cyanide to form a **hydroxynitrile** (which extends the carbon chain by one carbon).

*   **Reagents:** $HCN$ generated *in situ* using a mixture of **$KCN$ or $NaCN$ and dilute $H_2SO_4$**.
*   **Catalyst:** Trace amounts of $NaCN$ or $NaOH$ (alkali). $HCN$ is a weak acid and barely dissociates. The added cyanide salt provides the initial $CN^-$ nucleophiles to start the reaction.
*   **Conditions:** Heat under **reflux**.
*   **Overall Equation:** $CH_3CHO + HCN \rightarrow CH_3CH(OH)CN$ (2-hydroxypropanenitrile)

### 4.1 The Mechanism (MUST KNOW)
> [!abstract] Diagram: Mechanism for Nucleophilic Addition
> You must be able to draw this exactly as the mark scheme demands.
> 
> **Step 1: Nucleophilic Attack**
> *   Draw the polar bond: $C^{\delta+} = O^{\delta-}$.
> *   Draw the cyanide ion ($:CN^-$) showing the **lone pair on the carbon atom** and the negative charge.
> *   Draw a **curly arrow** starting exactly from the lone pair on the $C$ of $CN^-$ to the $C^{\delta+}$ of the carbonyl group.
> *   Draw a **curly arrow** from the middle of the $C=O$ double bond moving onto the $O^{\delta-}$ atom.
> 
> **Step 2: Protonation of the Intermediate**
> *   Draw the intermediate: a central carbon bonded to $R$, $H$ (or $R'$), $CN$, and a single-bonded $O^-$. Ensure the $O$ has a lone pair and a negative charge.
> *   Draw the $H-CN$ molecule with a dipole: $H^{\delta+} - CN^{\delta-}$.
> *   Draw a **curly arrow** from the lone pair on the $O^-$ to the $H^{\delta+}$ of the $HCN$ molecule.
> *   Draw a **curly arrow** from the $H-CN$ bond breaking onto the $CN$, which regenerates the $:CN^-$ catalyst. 
> *(Note: The $O^-$ attacking an $H^+$ from the acid or $H_2O$ is also acceptable in MS, but showing it attacking $H-CN$ perfectly demonstrates catalyst regeneration).*

### 4.2 Optical Isomerism & Racemic Mixtures
A classic synoptic question combines carbonyls with stereoisomerism. If the nucleophilic addition creates a chiral center (i.e., the starting carbonyl is an aldehyde other than methanal, or an asymmetric ketone), the product will be a **racemic mixture**.

> [!info] Exam Phrasing for Optical Isomerism Marks
> *   "The carbonyl carbon is $sp^2$ hybridised, so the molecule is **trigonal planar** around the carbonyl group."
> *   "The $CN^-$ nucleophile can attack from **above or below the plane** with **equal probability/chance**."
> *   "This produces a **racemic mixture**, which contains **equimolar amounts** of both enantiomers."
> *   "The resulting mixture **has no effect on plane-polarised light** (the optical activities cancel out)."

## 5. Testing and Identification 

### 5.1 Detecting the Carbonyl Group: 2,4-DNPH
Brady's Reagent (2,4-dinitrophenylhydrazine) is used to test for the general presence of an aldehyde or ketone.
*   **Observation:** Forms a **deep-orange or yellow precipitate**.
*   **Reaction Type:** Condensation (or nucleophilic addition-elimination). Water is eliminated.
*   **Identifying the specific Carbonyl (Crucial MS steps):**
    1.  Filter the orange precipitate.
    2.  **Recrystallise** to purify the derivative.
    3.  Determine its **melting point**.
    4.  Compare the melting point to a database/data booklet of known values to identify the original aldehyde or ketone.

### 5.2 Distinguishing Aldehydes from Ketones
Aldehydes can be easily oxidized; ketones cannot. We use mild oxidizing agents to tell them apart.

**A. Tollens’ Reagent (Ammoniacal Silver Nitrate)**
*   **Reagent:** $AgNO_3$ in aqueous ammonia. Contains the complex ion $[Ag(NH_3)_2]^+$.
*   **Conditions:** **Warm** in a water bath.
*   **Observation (Aldehyde):** A **silver mirror** forms on the inside of the test tube.
*   **Observation (Ketone):** No visible change.
*   **Ionic Equation:** $Ag^+$ is reduced to $Ag(s)$. The aldehyde is oxidized to a carboxylate ion (since the solution is alkaline).
    *   $RCHO + 2Ag^+ + 3OH^- \rightarrow RCOO^- + 2Ag + 2H_2O$

**B. Fehling’s Solution**
*   **Reagent:** Alkaline solution containing Copper(II) ions ($Cu^{2+}$) which is blue.
*   **Conditions:** **Warm** in a water bath.
*   **Observation (Aldehyde):** Blue solution turns into a **brick-red precipitate**.
*   **Observation (Ketone):** Remains blue.
*   **Ionic Equation:** $Cu^{2+}$ is reduced to $Cu^+$, forming $Cu_2O(s)$. 
    *   $RCHO + 2Cu^{2+} + 5OH^- \rightarrow RCOO^- + Cu_2O + 3H_2O$

### 5.3 Tri-iodomethane (Iodoform) Test
This test identifies the presence of the **$CH_3CO-$ (methyl carbonyl)** group. 
*By extension, it also tests for the $CH_3CH(OH)-$ group, as the oxidizing nature of the reagents will first oxidize the secondary alcohol into a methyl carbonyl.*

*   **Reagents:** Alkaline aqueous Iodine ($I_2$ + $NaOH(aq)$ or $KOH(aq)$). Warmed.
*   **Observation:** A **pale yellow precipitate** forms (this is tri-iodomethane, $CHI_3$, which has a distinct antiseptic smell).
*   **Target Molecules:**
    *   **Aldehydes:** Only **ethanal** ($CH_3CHO$) gives a positive result.
    *   **Ketones:** All **methyl ketones** (alkan-2-ones like propanone, butanone, pentan-2-one) give a positive result.
*   **Overall Equation:** The methyl group is severed from the chain to form $CHI_3$, and the remainder of the chain forms a carboxylic acid salt (one carbon shorter).
    *   $RCOCH_3 + 3I_2 + 4OH^- \rightarrow RCOO^- + CHI_3 + 3I^- + 3H_2O$

## 6. Top in the World Tips & Tricks 🏆

> [!tip] Examiner Expectations & Common Pitfalls
> 1. **"Identify the Reagent" Traps:** When asked for the reagent for reduction, if you write $LiAlH_4$, you **MUST** state the condition "**in dry ether**". If you just write $LiAlH_4$, examiners will often withhold the condition mark, or sometimes both! Safest bet for aqueous chemistry is $NaBH_4(aq)$.
> 2. **Mechanism Arrows:** The curly arrow from the cyanide ion MUST start exactly from the **lone pair** or the negative charge on the **Carbon atom**, not the Nitrogen atom. $:C \equiv N^-$
> 3. **The Iodoform Step-Down:** Exam questions love giving you an unknown compound that reacts with $I_2/NaOH$ to give a yellow ppt. Remember that the carboxylate salt left behind has **one less carbon** than the starting molecule! If they ask you to draw the salt, ensure it is the anion ($RCOO^-$) or sodium salt ($RCOO^-Na^+$), *not* the carboxylic acid ($RCOOH$), because the reaction is in an alkaline medium.
> 4. **Tollens' vs Fehling's:** While both test for aldehydes, Fehling's solution *does not* oxidize aromatic aldehydes (like benzaldehyde) efficiently. Tollens' is universally used for both aliphatic and aromatic aldehydes.
> 5. **Hydroxynitrile Hydrolysis (Synoptic link):** Remember that the $-C \equiv N$ group on a hydroxynitrile can be hydrolysed using dilute acid (e.g., $HCl(aq)$) and heat to form a **hydroxycarboxylic acid** ($-COOH$) + $NH_4^+$. This is a vital step in organic synthesis for extending a carbon chain by 1 and ending up with a carboxylic acid.
