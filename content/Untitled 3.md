# 📝 Exam Q&A Vault: Carboxylic Acids & Derivatives (CIE 9701)

> [!info] How to use this note
> These are the exact, high-yield questions extracted from past paper mark schemes (Papers 1, 2, and 4). The answers provided contain the specific keywords and phrasing that CIE examiners actively look for when awarding marks.

### 1. Structure and Physical Properties

**Q: Explain why carboxylic acids have higher boiling points than alkanes or alcohols of similar relative molecular mass.**
> **Mark Scheme Answer:**
> Carboxylic acids can form extensive intermolecular hydrogen bonds. In the gas phase or non-polar solvents, they form dimers (two molecules hydrogen-bonding together). This effectively doubles their apparent relative molecular mass ($M_r$), significantly increasing the van der Waals' (London dispersion) forces between the dimers, meaning more energy is required to break the intermolecular forces.

### 2. Preparation & Hydrolysis 

**Q: Write the general balanced equation for the acid hydrolysis of a nitrile ($R-CN$) to a carboxylic acid.**
> **Mark Scheme Answer:**
> $R-CN + 2H_2O + H^+ \rightarrow R-COOH + NH_4^+$

**Q: Explain why a strong dilute acid must be added after the alkaline hydrolysis of a nitrile or an ester.**
> **Mark Scheme Answer:**
> Alkaline hydrolysis produces the carboxylate salt (e.g., $R-COO^-Na^+$) rather than the free acid. The strong acid is required to protonate the carboxylate ion and displace/release the free carboxylic acid ($R-COOH$).

**Q: State the reagents and conditions required to prepare benzoic acid from methylbenzene.**
> **Mark Scheme Answer:**
> Heat under reflux with hot alkaline potassium manganate(VII) ($KMnO_4$), followed by acidification with dilute acid (e.g., $HCl_{aq}$).

**Q: What is the organic product formed when propylbenzene is heated under reflux with alkaline $KMnO_4$ followed by acidification?**
> **Mark Scheme Answer:**
> Benzoic acid. *(Examiner note: The entire alkyl side-chain, regardless of length, is oxidised down to a single -COOH group attached to the benzene ring).*

### 3. Reactions of Carboxylic Acids

**Q: Describe a chemical test, including the expected observation, that distinguishes a carboxylic acid from an alcohol or a phenol.**
> **Mark Scheme Answer:**
> Add aqueous sodium carbonate ($Na_2CO_3$) or sodium hydrogencarbonate ($NaHCO_3$). Carboxylic acids will produce effervescence/bubbles of $CO_2$ gas. Alcohols and phenols will show no reaction.

**Q: Which of the following will react with sodium carbonate: alcohols, phenols, or carboxylic acids?**
> **Mark Scheme Answer:**
> Only carboxylic acids.

**Q: State the reagents and conditions required to reduce a carboxylic acid to a primary alcohol.**
> **Mark Scheme Answer:**
> Lithium aluminium hydride ($LiAlH_4$) in dry ether at room temperature.

**Q: Explain why $NaBH_4$ cannot be used to reduce a carboxylic acid.**
> **Mark Scheme Answer:**
> $NaBH_4$ is a weaker reducing agent and is not strong enough to reduce the highly oxidised carboxylic acid group.

### 4. Further Oxidation Exceptions

**Q: Name the two specific carboxylic acids that can undergo further oxidation.**
> **Mark Scheme Answer:**
> Methanoic acid ($HCOOH$) and Ethanedioic acid ($HOOC-COOH$).

**Q: Explain structurally why methanoic acid can be oxidised further, but ethanoic acid cannot.**
> **Mark Scheme Answer:**
> Methanoic acid has a hydrogen atom attached directly to the carbonyl carbon (creating an aldehyde-like group, -CHO). This allows it to act as a reducing agent and be oxidised further. Ethanoic acid has a methyl group instead, which cannot be oxidised.

**Q: State the observations when methanoic acid is warmed with Tollens' reagent and Fehling's solution.**
> **Mark Scheme Answer:**
> - **Tollens' reagent:** A silver mirror forms. 
> - **Fehling's solution:** A brick-red precipitate forms.

**Q: Write the balanced ionic equation for the redox reaction between ethanedioic acid and acidified $KMnO_4$.**
> **Mark Scheme Answer:**
> $5H_2C_2O_4 + 2MnO_4^- + 6H^+ \rightarrow 10CO_2 + 2Mn^{2+} + 8H_2O$

**Q: Explain why the titration between ethanedioic acid and $KMnO_4$ is initially very slow but speeds up as the reaction proceeds.**
> **Mark Scheme Answer:**
> The reaction is autocatalysed. The $Mn^{2+}$ ions produced during the reaction act as a catalyst for the reaction.

### 5. Explaining Acidity (Paper 4 Favourites)

**Q: State the relative order of acidity for carboxylic acids, phenols, water, and alcohols (from strongest to weakest).**
> **Mark Scheme Answer:**
> Carboxylic acids > Phenols > Water > Alcohols.

**Q: Explain why carboxylic acids are stronger acids than alcohols and phenols.**
> **Mark Scheme Answer:**
> When a carboxylic acid loses a proton, it forms a carboxylate anion. The negative charge is **delocalised over the two highly electronegative oxygen atoms** of the $COO^-$ group. This reduces the charge density and highly stabilises the anion, making it much less likely to attract the $H^+$ ion back compared to alkoxide or phenoxide ions.

**Q: Explain why phenol is a weaker acid than a carboxylic acid but a stronger acid than an alcohol.**
> **Mark Scheme Answer:**
> In phenol, the lone pair of electrons on the oxygen atom delocalises into the $\pi$-system of the benzene ring, stabilising the phenoxide ion (making it stronger than an alcohol). However, it is weaker than a carboxylic acid because the delocalisation in a carboxylate ion occurs over two highly electronegative oxygen atoms, making the carboxylate ion much more stable than the phenoxide ion.

**Q: Explain the effect of electron-withdrawing groups (e.g., chlorine atoms) on the acid strength of a carboxylic acid.**
> **Mark Scheme Answer:**
> Electronegative atoms exert a **negative inductive effect**, pulling electron density away from the -COOH group. This weakens the O-H bond and further spreads out/delocalises the negative charge on the resulting carboxylate anion. This stabilises the anion and increases acid strength.

**Q: Place the following acids in order of increasing acid strength: chloroethanoic acid, ethanoic acid, trichloroethanoic acid, dichloroethanoic acid.**
> **Mark Scheme Answer:**
> Ethanoic acid < Chloroethanoic acid < Dichloroethanoic acid < Trichloroethanoic acid.

**Q: Explain why trichloroethanoic acid is a stronger acid than chloroethanoic acid.**
> **Mark Scheme Answer:**
> Three highly electronegative chlorine atoms exert a greater electron-withdrawing (negative inductive) effect than just one. This pulls more electron density away from the $COO^-$ group, spreading out the negative charge more effectively. This makes the anion more stable and less likely to attract back the $H^+$ ion.

**Q: Explain why 2-chlorobutanoic acid is a stronger acid than 4-chlorobutanoic acid.**
> **Mark Scheme Answer:**
> The closer the electronegative chlorine atom is to the -COOH group, the stronger its electron-withdrawing (negative inductive) effect on the carboxylate ion. Therefore, the conjugate base of 2-chlorobutanoic acid is more stabilised.

**Q: Explain why ethanoic acid is a weaker acid than methanoic acid.**
> **Mark Scheme Answer:**
> The methyl group in ethanoic acid is electron-donating (**positive inductive effect**). This pushes electron density towards the -COOH group, increasing the charge density on the carboxylate anion, destabilising it, and making it more likely to accept an $H^+$ ion.

### 6. Acyl Chlorides: Preparation, Reactivity & Mechanisms

**Q: State three different reagents that can be used to convert a carboxylic acid into an acyl chloride.**
> **Mark Scheme Answer:**
> 1. Solid Phosphorus(V) chloride ($PCl_5$).
> 2. Liquid Phosphorus(III) chloride ($PCl_3$) + heat.
> 3. Liquid Sulfur dichloride oxide ($SOCl_2$).

**Q: Explain why $SOCl_2$ is often the preferred reagent for preparing acyl chlorides in industry.**
> **Mark Scheme Answer:**
> The by-products of the reaction with $SOCl_2$ ($SO_2$ and $HCl$) are both gases. They easily escape the reaction mixture, leaving behind the pure liquid acyl chloride, eliminating the need for complex separation/purification.

**Q: What visual observation is consistently made when an acyl chloride reacts with any nucleophile (e.g., water, alcohol, ammonia)?**
> **Mark Scheme Answer:**
> Steamy white fumes (of $HCl$ gas) are observed.

**Q: State three advantages of using an acyl chloride rather than a carboxylic acid to make an ester.**
> **Mark Scheme Answer:**
> 1. The reaction goes to completion (higher yield), whereas reaction with a carboxylic acid is an equilibrium.
> 2. The reaction occurs rapidly at room temperature (does not require heating under reflux).
> 3. It does not require a concentrated acid catalyst.

**Q: Explain why NaOH is added to phenol before reacting it with an acyl chloride to make a phenyl ester.**
> **Mark Scheme Answer:**
> NaOH reacts with phenol to form the phenoxide ion ($C_6H_5O^-$). The phenoxide ion is a stronger/better nucleophile than phenol itself, allowing the addition-elimination reaction with the acyl chloride to occur more readily.

**Q: Explain why two moles of ammonia (or amine) are required per mole of acyl chloride in the formation of an amide.**
> **Mark Scheme Answer:**
> The first mole acts as a nucleophile to attack the acyl chloride and form the amide, producing $HCl$ as a by-product. The second mole acts as a base to neutralise the highly acidic $HCl$, forming an ammonium salt (e.g., $NH_4Cl$).

**Q: Name the mechanism by which acyl chlorides react with nucleophiles.**
> **Mark Scheme Answer:**
> Nucleophilic addition-elimination (also accepted: condensation).

**Q: Explain why acyl chlorides are much more reactive than alkyl chlorides (halogenoalkanes).**
> **Mark Scheme Answer:**
> In an acyl chloride, the carbonyl carbon is bonded to two highly electronegative atoms (Oxygen and Chlorine). Both pull electron density away, leaving the carbon intensely electron-deficient (large $\delta+$ charge) and highly susceptible to rapid nucleophilic attack. Alkyl chlorides only have one electronegative atom (Cl) attached.

**Q: State the relative order of ease of hydrolysis (reactivity with water) for acyl chlorides, alkyl chlorides, and aryl chlorides (chlorobenzene).**
> **Mark Scheme Answer:**
> Acyl chloride > Alkyl chloride > Aryl chloride (chlorobenzene).

**Q: Explain why aryl chlorides (chlorobenzene) are extremely resistant to hydrolysis / nucleophilic attack.**
> **Mark Scheme Answer:**
> A lone pair of electrons on the chlorine atom delocalises into the $\pi$-electron system of the benzene ring. This overlap gives the $C-Cl$ bond **partial double-bond character**, making it much stronger and harder to break compared to a standard $C-Cl$ bond.
