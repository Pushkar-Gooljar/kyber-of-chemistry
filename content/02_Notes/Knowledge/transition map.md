# Topic 28: Chemistry of the Transition Elements

## 28.1 General Physical and Chemical Properties

> [!info] Definition of a Transition Element (2 marks)
> Define a transition element.

> [!info] Electronic Configurations of Atoms and Ions (2 marks)
> Complete the electronic configuration for an isolated gaseous copper atom, Cu, and a copper(II) ion, $Cu^{2+}$.

> [!info] Typical Chemical Properties (2 marks)
> Transition elements such as iron and copper exhibit specific chemical properties not seen in s-block elements. State two typical chemical properties of a transition element.

> [!info] Shape of d-orbitals (2 marks)
> There are five different 3d orbitals. Sketch the shapes of a $3d_{xy}$ orbital and a $3d_{z^2}$ orbital on the axes provided.

> [!info] Variable Oxidation States (2 marks)
> Explain why transition elements have variable oxidation states in their compounds, whereas s-block elements such as calcium do not.

> [!info] Complex Ion Formation (2 marks)
> Explain, in terms of d-orbitals, why transition elements are able to form complex ions.

> [!info] Catalytic Behaviour (2 marks)
> Explain why transition elements and their compounds behave as catalysts.

> [!info] Modes of Action of Catalysts (3 marks)
> Iron acts as a heterogeneous catalyst in the Haber process, whereas $Fe^{2+}$ ions act as a homogeneous catalyst in the reaction between $I^-$ and $S_2O_8^{2-}$. 
> 1. Explain the difference between a homogeneous catalyst and a heterogeneous catalyst.
> 2. Describe the mode of action of a heterogeneous iron catalyst.

> [!info] Homogeneous Catalysis Equations (2 marks)
> Write equations for the two reactions that occur when $Fe^{2+}(aq)$ is added to a mixture of $I^-(aq)$ and $S_2O_8^{2-}(aq)$ to act as a homogeneous catalyst.

## 28.2 Characteristic Chemistry and Ligands

> [!info] Identifying Ligand Donation (2 marks)

> [!info] Deducing Complex Formulae and Charge (2 marks)

> [!info] Copper(II) Aqueous Reactions: Precipitation vs Ligand Exchange (6 marks)

> [!info] Cobalt(II) Aqueous Reactions (6 marks)

> [!info] Redox Reactions: Manganate(VII) and Ethanedioate (4 marks)

> [!info] Redox Reactions: Copper(II) and Iodide (4 marks)

> [!info] Predicting Redox Feasibility using $E^\theta$ Data (3 marks)

## 28.5 Stability Constants, $K_{stab}$

> [!info] Definition of Stability Constant (2 marks)
> Define the term *stability constant*, $K_{stab}$, of a complex.

> [!info] Writing $K_{stab}$ Expressions (2 marks)
> When $NH_3(aq)$ is added to $[Cu(H_2O)_6]^{2+}$, the complex $[Cu(NH_3)_4(H_2O)_2]^{2+}$ is formed. 
> Write an expression for the stability constant, $K_{stab}$, for this equilibrium and deduce its units.

> [!info] Interpreting $K_{stab}$ Values (2 marks)
> Table 1 shows the stability constants for various silver(I) complexes:
> $[Ag(CN)_2]^-$ ($K_{stab} = 1.1 \times 10^{18}$)
> $[Ag(NH_3)_2]^+$ ($K_{stab} = 1.2 \times 10^7$)
> Use this information to identify the most stable silver(I) complex and explain your reasoning.

> [!info] Ligand Exchange and $K_{stab}$ (2 marks)
> An aqueous solution containing $[Ca(EDTA)]^{2-}$ ($K_{stab} = 5.0 \times 10^{10}$) is added to a solution containing equal concentrations of $Pb^{2+}$ ions. The complex $[Pb(EDTA)]^{2-}$ has a $K_{stab}$ of $1.1 \times 10^{18}$. 
> Predict what reaction, if any, will occur. Explain your answer with reference to the $K_{stab}$ values.

> [!info] Calculating Equilibrium Concentrations using $K_{stab}$ (3 marks)
> In a particular solution at equilibrium, the concentration of $[Cu(NH_3)_4(H_2O)_2]^{2+}$ is $0.0074 \text{ mol dm}^{-3}$ and the concentration of $NH_3$ is $0.57 \text{ mol dm}^{-3}$. 
> Given that $K_{stab} = 1.40 \times 10^{13} \text{ mol}^{-4} \text{ dm}^{12}$, calculate the concentration of the $[Cu(H_2O)_6]^{2+}$ complex ion in this solution. Show your working.
