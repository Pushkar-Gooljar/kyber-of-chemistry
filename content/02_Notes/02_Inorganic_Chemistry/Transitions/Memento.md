# General

>[!info] Definition: Transition Element
>- A d-block element which forms one or more stable ions with incomplete d-orbitals.

>[!info] Chemical Properties of Transition Elements 
>1. Variable oxidation states 
>2. Act as catalysts
>3. Form coloured compounds
>4. Form complex ions

>[!info] Why Transition Elements have Variable Oxidation States
>- The 3d and 4s sub-shells are close in energy.

>[!tip] Why calcium does not show variable oxidation states
>- The energy gap between the outer s-orbital and the next inner orbital is too large

>[!info] Electronic configuration of \[Ar]
>$$\Large\mathrm{1s^22s^22p^63s^23p^6}$$


>[!warning] Electronic configuration of copper
>$$\Large\mathrm{[Ar]3d^{10}4s^1}$$

>[!info] Why electronic configuration of copper is not $\mathrm{[Ar]3d^{9}4s^{2}}$
>- The fully-filled 3d subshell offers extra stability 


>[!warning] Electronic configuration of chromium
>$$\Large\mathrm{[Ar]3d^{5}4s^1}$$

>[!info] Why electronic configuration of copper is not $\mathrm{[Ar]3d^{6}4s^{0}}$
>- The half-filled 3d subshell offers extra stability

# Catalysis

>[!info] Why do transition elements behave as catalysts 
>1. They have more than one stable oxidation state.
>2. They have vacant d-orbitals that are energetically accessible to accept lone pairs to form dative bonds with ligands.

# Ligands and Complexes

>[!info] Definition: Complex ion
>- A species formed by a central metal ion bonded to one or more ligands.

>[!info] Definition: Ligand
>- A species that contains lone pairs of electrons that form dative bonds with a central metal ion.

>[!info] Definition: Monodentate Ligand
>- A species that uses one lone pair of electrons to form a single dative bond to a central metal atom.

>[!info] Definition: Bidentate Ligand
>- A species that uses two lone pairs of electrons to form two dative bonds to a central metal atom.

>[!info] Definition: Polydentate Ligand
>- A species that donates more than two lone pairs of electrons 
>- to form more than two dative bonds to a central metal atom

>[!info] Definition: Coordination number
>- Total number of coordinate bonds being formed by the metal ion.

>[!info] Why transition elements form complex ions
>- They have vacant d-orbitals that are energetically accessible to accept lone pairs from ligands.

# Reactions of $\ce{Cu^2+}$

## Add dropwise $\ce{NaOH}$ or $\ce{NH_{3}}$
>[!success] Product
>$$\Large\ce{Cu(H_{2}O)4(OH)2}$$

>[!mechanism] Equation (Dropwise $\ce{NaOH_{(aq)}}$)
>$$\large \underbrace{\ce{[Cu(H2O)6]^2+}}_{\text{(aq) Pale blue solution}} + \ce{2OH^- ->} \underbrace{\ce{[Cu(H2O)4(OH)2]}}_{\text{(s) Pale blue precipitate}} + \ce{2H2O}$$

>[!mechanism] Equation (Dropwise dil. $\ce{NH_{3}_{(aq)}}$)
>$$\large \underbrace{\ce{[Cu(H2O)6]^2+}}_{\text{(aq) Pale blue solution}} + \ce{2NH3 ->} \underbrace{\ce{[Cu(H2O)4(OH)2]}}_{\text{(s) Pale blue precipitate}} + \ce{2NH4^+}$$



>[!info] Type of Reaction
>- Deprotonation 
>- Acid-Base
>- Precipitation

>[!warning] 
><p class="emphasis">⚠️ THIS IS NOT A LIGAND EXCHANGE REACTION ⚠️</p>
>
>![Copper(II) Hexaaqua + Dropwise NaOH|500](copper_ii_hexaaqua_getting_graped.svg)
>
>When a few drops of $\ce{NaOH_{(aq)}}$ or dilute $\ce{NH_{3}_{(aq)}}$ are added to hexaaquacopper(II), **no $\ce{Cu - O}$ coordinate bonds are broken**.
>
>- **The Mechanism**: The incoming $\ce{OH-}$ (or $\ce{NH_{3}}$) acts strictly as a **Brønsted-Lowry base**, accepting protons ($\ce{H+}$) from two coordinated water molecules.
>  </br>
>- **In-situ conversion:** The coordinated $\ce{H_{2}O}$ ligands are deprotonated directly on the central metal atom to become $\ce{OH-}$ ligands.
>  </br>
>- **The $+2\text{H}_2\text{O}$ product:** The water molecules formed on the product side of the $\text{NaOH}$ reaction are generated by **acid–base neutralisation** ($\text{H}^+ + \text{OH}^- \rightarrow \text{H}_2\text{O}$), **not** displaced ligands leaving the coordination sphere!
