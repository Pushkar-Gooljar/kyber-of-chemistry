# Definitions 
>[!info] Definition: Ligand
>A species that contains a **lone pair of electrons** that forms a dative covalent bond with a central metal ion.

>[!info] Definition: Complex
>A species or ion formed by a central metal ion surrounded by one or more ligands.

>[!info] Definition: Coordination Number
>The number of coordinate bonds formed between the ligands and central metal ion.
>
>>[!warning]
>>It is NOT just the number of ligands!

>[!example] Example of a Complex Ion
>![Complex Ion Structure|300](https://s3.eu-west-2.amazonaws.com/elements.cognitoedu.org/4c4c6424-a062-4d0d-b01b-4a98d32ea20d/ED1%402x%20%2813%29.png)
>
>**In this case:**
>- **Ligand:** $\ce{H_{2}O}$
>- **Complex:** $[\ce{Cr(H_{2}O)6}]^{3+}$
>- **Coordination Number:** $6$



# Types of Ligands
## Monodentate Ligand

>[!info] Monodentate Ligand
> Species that donates **one lone pair** of electrons to form a **single dative covalent** bond to a central **metal ion**.

>[!example] Examples of Monodentate Ligands
>$\ce{Cl-}$ | $\ce{H_{2}O}$ | $\ce{NH_{3}}$ | $\ce{CN-}$
>
>![Examples of Monodentate ligands.|400](https://cdn.savemyexams.com/uploads/2021/02/6.2-Chemistry-of-Transition-Elements-Monodentate-Ligands.png)

## Bidentate Ligand
>[!info] Bidentate Ligand
>Species that donates two lone pairs of electrons to form dative covalent bonds to a central metal ion

>[!example] Examples of Bidentate Ligands
>- 1,2-diaminoethane: $H_2NCH_2CH_2NH_2$ abbreviated as **en**
>- Ethanedioate: $C_2O_4^{2-}$ abbreviated as **ox**
>  
>![Examples of Bidentate ligands.|400](https://cdn.savemyexams.com/uploads/2021/02/6.2-Chemistry-of-Transition-Elements-Bidentate-Ligands.png)

## Polydentate Ligands
>[!info] Polydentate Ligand
>A species that donates more than two lone pairs of electrons to form dative covalent bonds to a central metal ion.

>[!example] Example of Multidentate Ligand
>- Ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid abbreviated as **EDTA$^{-4}$** (Hexadentate - forms 6 coordinate bonds)
>  
>  ![EDTA|400](https://cdn.savemyexams.com/uploads/2021/02/6.2-Chemistry-of-Transition-Elements-Polydentate-Ligands_2.png)

# Geometry of Complex Ions
>[!concept] Shape of Complexes
>The shape of a complex depends on its coordination number and the size of the ligands.
>
>**Coordination Number 6 (Octahedral)** 
>- Formed with small ligands like $\ce{H_{2}O}$ and $\ce{NH_{3}}$
>- Bond angle: **90°**
>- Example: $[Cu(H_2O)_6]^{2+}$
>  
>**Coordination Number 4 (Tetrahedral)**
>- Formed with larger, repelling ligands like $\ce{Cl-}$
>- Bond angle: **109.5°**
>- Example: $[CuCl_4]^{2-}$
>  
>**Coordination Number 4 (Square Planar)**
>- Commonly seen with $\ce{Pt^2+}$ and $\ce{Ni^2+}$
>- Bond angle: **90°**
>- Example: Cisplatin $[Pt(NH_3)_2Cl_2]$
>
>**Coordination Number 2 (Linear)**
>- Common with $\ce{Ag+}$ and $\ce{Cu+}$
>- Bond angle: **180°**
>- Example: Tollens' reagent $[Ag(NH_3)_2]^+$
