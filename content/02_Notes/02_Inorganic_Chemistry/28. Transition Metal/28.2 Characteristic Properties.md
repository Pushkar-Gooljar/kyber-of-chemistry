>[!info] Characteristic Properties of Transition Elements
>Transition elements have 4 distinct properties that distinguish them from s-block metals:
>1. [[28.2 Characteristic Properties#Variable Oxidation States|Variable Oxidation States]]
>2. [[28.2 Characteristic Properties#Behaviour as Catalysts|Behave as Catalysts]]
>3. [[28.2 Characteristic Properties#Formation of Complex Ions|Form Complex Ions]]
>4. Form Coloured Compounds

# Variable Oxidation States
>[!concept] Why do Transition Elements have Variable Oxidation States?
>- The $\mathrm{3d}$ and $\mathrm{4s}$ subshells are similar in energy.
>- Electrons can be lost from both sub-shells with relatively similar amounts of ionisation energy.

# Behaviour as Catalysts
>[!concept] Why do Transition Elements act as Catalysts?
>- **They have more than one stable oxidation state.** 
>(allowing them to act as homogenous catalysts by transferring electrons)
></br>
>- **They have vacant d orbitals that are energetically accessible allowing them to form dative bonds with ligands.**
>(acting as heterogeneous catalysts by adsorbing reactants).

# Formation of Complex Ions
>[!concept] Why Transition Elements can form Complex Ions?
>- The possess **vacant d orbitals that are energetically accessible**
>(Allowing them to accept lone pairs of electrons from ligands to form dative covalent bonds.)
