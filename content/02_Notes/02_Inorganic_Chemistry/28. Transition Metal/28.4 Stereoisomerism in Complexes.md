# Geometrical Isomerism
>[!info] Geometrical Isomerism
>Only occurs in **Square Planar** and **Octahedral** complexes where there are pairs of different ligands.
>
>**Cis-isomer**
>- Identical ligands are adjacent to each other (90°). 
>- **Usually Polar** due to an imbalance in charge distribution (dipoles do not cancel).
>
>**Trans-isomer**
>- Identical ligands are opposite to each other (180° apart).
>- **Usually Non-Polar** because the dipoles perfectly cancel each other out.


## Square Planar Geometric Isomerism
>[!info] Square Planar Geometric Isomerism
>- Only with monodentate ligands
>- Complexes of type $\mathrm{Ma_{2}b_{2}}$

>[!example] Example: $\ce{PtCl_{2}(NH_{3})2}$

## Octahedral Geometric Isomerism
>[!info] Octahedral Geometric Isomerism
>**With Monodentate Ligands:**
>- $\mathrm{Ma_{2}b_{4}}$
>
>**With Bidentate Ligands**
>- $\mathrm{Ma_{2}(b-b)_{2}}$

>[!example] Example 1: $\ce{Ni(NH_{3})4Cl_{2}}$

# Optical Isomerism
>[!info] Optical Isomerism
>- Only occurs in octahedral complexes with at least 2 bidentate ligands.
>- The isomers have identical chemical and physical properties but rotate plane polarised light in opposite directions.

>[!example] Example: $[\ce{Ni(en)3}]^{3+}$
