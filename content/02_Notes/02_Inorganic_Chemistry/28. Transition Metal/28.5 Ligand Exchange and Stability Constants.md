# Ligand Exchange
>[!info] Ligand Exchange 
>- **Ligand Exchange** (or ligand substitution) is when one **ligand** in a complex is replaced by another.
>- This happens if the new complex formed is **more stable** than the original.
>- The ligands in the original complex can be **partially** or **entirely** substituted by others.

>[!warning] Changes in Coordination Number and Geometry
>- There are no changes in coordination number, or the geometry of the complex if the ligands are of a **similar size**.
>  </br>
>- But if the ligands are of a **different size**, a change in coordination number and the geometry of the complex will occur.

# Key Ligand Exchange Reactions
## $\ce{Cu^2+}$ Complexes
>[!info] 
>- When a transition element ion is in aqueous solution, it can be assumed to exist in a **hexaaqua** complex ion (i.e. It has six water ligands attached to it)
>- For example, $\ce{Cu^2+(aq)}$ exists as $\ce{[Cu(H_{2}O)6]^2+(aq)}$ complex ion.
>- $\ce{[Cu(H_{2}O)6]^2+(aq)}$ is blue in colour.


>[!concept] Dropwise Addition of $\ce{OH-}$ or $\ce{NH_{3}}$ **(NOT LIGAND EXCHANGE!)**
>**Reactant:** $\ce{[Cu(H_{2}O)6]^2+(aq)}$
>
>**Reagent:** $\ce{NaOH(aq)}$ or $\ce{NH_{3}(aq)}$ added **dropwise**.
>
>**Observations:** Pale blue precipitate.
>
>**Complex Formed:** $\ce{[Cu(H_{2}O)4(OH)2](s)}$ (Neutral and Insoluble)
>
>**Type of Reaction:** Acid-Base / Deprotonation / Precipitation
>
>**Reactions:**
>
>- **With $\ce{OH-}$**
>$$\ce{\underbrace{ [Cu(H2O)6]^{2+}(aq) }_{ \text{Blue Solution} } + 2OH^-(aq) -> \underbrace{ [Cu(H2O)4(OH)2](s) }_{ \text{Pale Blue Ppt} } + 2H2O(l)}$$
>
>- **With $\ce{NH_{3}}$**
>$$\ce{\underbrace{ [Cu(H2O)6]^{2+}(aq) }_{ \text{Blue Solution} } + 2NH3(aq) -> \underbrace{ [Cu(H2O)4(OH)2](s) }_{ \text{Pale Blue Ppt} } + 2NH4+(aq)}$$
>
>>[!success] Explanation
>>**Acid-Base Mechanism**
>>- The $\ce{[Cu(H2O)6]^{2+}}$ acts as a **Brønsted-Lowry acid** (proton donor).
>>- The $\ce{OH-}$ acts as a **Brønsted-Lowry base** (proton acceptor).
>>- The $\ce{OH-}$ removes $\ce{H+}$ directly from two of the water ligands.
>>
>>**No Ligand Exchange**
>>- The $\ce{OH-}$ in the product ligand is not from the added $\ce{OH-}$, it is what remains after $\ce{H+}$ was removed from two of the $\ce{H_{2}O}$ ligands.
>>- The $\ce{H_{2}O}$ produced is not from the original complex, it was produced by the neutralisation of the $\ce{OH-}$ added and the $\ce{H+}$ donated by the complex.
>>
>>**Reaction of $\ce{NH_{3}}$**
>>- The $\ce{NH_{3}}$ first reacts with water to form $\ce{OH-}$:
>>$$\ce{NH_{3} + H_{2}O \rightleftharpoons OH- + NH_{4}+}$$
>>
>>**Solubility**
>>- The original complex was soluble because it had a +2 charge, meaning that it could form strong ion-dipole bonds with water molecules.
>>- The resulting complex is uncharged, meaning that it cannot form ion-dipole bonds effectively. It is therefore insoluble.



>[!concept] Addition of $\ce{NH_{3}}$ in Excess (**Ligand Exchange (Partial)**)
>**Reactant:** $\ce{[Cu(H_{2}O)4(OH)2](s)}$
>
>**Reagent:** Excess $\ce{NH_{3}(aq)}$
>
>**Observations:** Light blue precipitate dissolves to give **A dark blue solution**
>
>**Complex Formed:** $\ce{[Cu(NH_{3})4(H_{2}O)2]^2+(aq)}$
>
>**Type of Reaction:** Ligand Exchange ($\ce{OH-}$ and $\ce{H_{2}O}$ $\ce{->}$ $\ce{NH_{3}}$)
>
>**Reaction:**
>$$\ce{[Cu(H_{2}O)4(OH)2](s) + 4NH_{3}(aq) -> [Cu(NH_{3})4(H_{2}O)2]^2+(aq) + 2H_{2}O(l) + 2OH-(aq)}$$
>
>>[!success] Explanation
>>**Reactant before substitution:**
>>- The reactant was $\ce{[Cu(H_{2}O)4(OH)2]}$
>>  
>>**What got substituted:**
>>- The two $\ce{OH-}$ ligands got substituted by two $\ce{NH_{3}}$ ligands.
>>- Two of the four $\ce{H_{2}O}$ ligands got substituted by two $\ce{NH_{3}}$ ligands.
>>
>>**Solubility**
>>- The resulting complex has a +2 charge ($\ce{NH_{3}}$ and $\ce{H_{2}O}$ are uncharged + $\ce{Cu^2+}$)
>>- Since it is charged, it can easily form ion-dipole bonds with water molecules. It is therefore soluble.
>>- Hence why the precipitate dissolves to form a dark blue solution.
