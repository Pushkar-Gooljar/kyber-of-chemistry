# Coloured Complexes

>[!concept] Why are Transition Element Complexes Coloured?
>1. In an isolated transition metal ion, the 5 d-orbitals are **degenerate**.
> 2. When ligands bond to the central metal ion, the repulsion from the ligands' lone pairs causes **d-orbital splitting** into two **non-degenerate** sets of orbitals with an energy gap, $\Delta E$.
> 3. An electron absorbs energy in the form of a specific frequency of visible light and is promoted from the lower energy d-orbital to the higher energy d-orbital.
> 4. The **colour observed is complementary** to the colour of light absorbed.

>[!success] Model Answer
>**Explain why transition elements can form coloured complexes. \[3]**
>
>The d-subshell splits into two different energy levels. When visible light passes through, specific frequencies are absorbed. The absorbed energy promotes electrons from a lower to a higher energy d-orbital. The colour observed is the complementary colour of the absorbed light, consisting of the remaining frequencies of visible light.

# Factors Affecting Colour (Changing $\Delta E$)
Any change to $\Delta E$ changes the frequency of light absorbed, thus altering the complementary colour observed. $\Delta E$ is changed by:
1.  **Different Ligands:** Ligands split the d-orbitals by different amounts (Spectrochemical series: $NH_3$ causes a larger split than $H_2O$, which is larger than $Cl^-$).
2.  **Different Coordination Number/Geometry:** Octahedral vs Tetrahedral splitting.
3.  **Different Metal Ion** or **Different Oxidation State:** Changes the nuclear charge and number of d-electrons.
