# Colour Of Complexes

>[!success] Explain why transition elements can form coloured complexes
>- Repulsion between d-electrons and lone pairs on ligands cause the d-subshell to split into two sets of non degenerate d-orbitals.
>- An electron from the lower energy d-orbital absorbs a photon having a specific frequency of visible light and is promoted to a higher energy d-orbital.
>- The absorbed frequency is missing from the transmitted light therefore the complementary colour is seen.

# d-d Splitting Due to Ligands
>[!info] Isolated Metal Ion
>In an isolated metal ion, the $3d$ orbitals are **degenerate**.
>
>![3d orbitals have the same energy (degenerate)|](degen-3d-orbitals.svg)


## Splitting in Tetrahedral Complexes
>[!info] Tetrahedral Molecular Geometry
>![Tetrahedral molecular geometry|200](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/f/fb/Tetrahedral-3D-balls.png)
>
>**Key features:**
>- **4** ligands attached to a central metal ion.
>- Bond angle of $109.5^{\circ}$
>  
> ![Tetrahedral shape from cube|200](https://www.stolaf.edu/people/hansonr/chem123/mahmud/cube.gif)
> 
> The tetrahedral shape can be thought of as resulting from alternating corners of a cube.

>[!important] How ligands approach central metal ion in tetrahedral complexes
>![Approach of ligands in a tetrahedral complex|400](tetrahedral-3d-orbitals.svg)
>
>**Direction of Ligand Approach**
>- In a tetrahedral shape, four ligands approach the central metal ion from alternating corners of a cube containing the metal ion at its centre.
>- Importantly, these directions **do not lie along the $x$, $y$ and $z$ axes.** Instead, the ligands approach **in between the axes.**
>
>**Electrostatic Interaction**
>- The lobes of the $3d_{{xy}}$, $3d_{{xz}}$ and $3d_{yz}$ orbitals point in the regions between the axes. Therefore, they point **much closer to the approaching ligands**. This results in **greater electrostatic repulsion** for these 3 orbitals.
>- The $3d_{x^2-y^2}$ and $3d_{z^2}$ orbitals point along the axes, which are further away from the path of the incoming ligands. They experience **less repulsion**
>


>[!success] Resulting Energy Levels 
>- Due to more repulsion from ligands, the $3d_{{xy}}$, $3d_{{xz}}$ and $3d_{yz}$ orbitals are at a higher energy level.
>- Due to less repulsion from ligands, the $3d_{x^2-y^2}$ and $3d_{z^2}$ are at a lower energy level.
>  
>  ![d-d splitting in tetrahedral complexes|500](https://cdn.savemyexams.com/cdn-cgi/image/f=auto,width=1920/https://cdn.savemyexams.com/uploads/2021/02/6.2-Chemistry-of-Transition-Elements-Splitting-in-tetrahedral-complex.png)


## Splitting in Octahedral Complexes
>[!info] Octahedral Molecular geometry
>![Octahedral molecular geometry|400](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1a/Octahedral-3D-balls.png/960px-Octahedral-3D-balls.png?_=20061212175404)
>
>**Key features**
>- $90^{\circ}$ bond angle
>- Shape coincides with Cartesian axes ($x$, $y$, $z$)

>[!important] How ligands approach central metal ion in octahedral complexes
>![Approach of ligands in octahedral complex|500](octahedral-3d-orbitals.svg)
>
>**Direction of Ligand Approach**
>- In an octahedral shape, six ligands approach the central metal ion **directly along the $x$, $y$ and $z$ axes**
>  
> **Electrostatic Interaction**
> - The lobes of the **$3d_{x^2-y^2}$** and **$3d_{z^2}$** orbitals point **directly at** the approaching ligands. This results in **maximum electrostatic repulsion**.
> - The lobes of the **$3d_{xy}$**, **$3d_{xz}$**, and **$3d_{yz}$** orbitals point **between** the axes, away from the direct line of approach of the ligands. They experience **less repulsion**.

>[!success] Resulting Energy Levels 
>>- Due to more repulsion from ligands, the $3d_{x^2-y^2}$ and $3d_{z^2}$ are at a higher energy level.
>- Due to less repulsion from ligands, the $3d_{{xy}}$, $3d_{{xz}}$ and $3d_{yz}$ orbitals are at a lower energy level.
>
