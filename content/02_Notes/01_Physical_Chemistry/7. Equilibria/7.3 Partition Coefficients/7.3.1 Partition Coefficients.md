## Definition

>[!info] Definition: Partition Coefficient
>- Ratio of the concentration of a solute in two **immiscible** solvents in contact with each other
>- At equilibrium
>  At a particular temperature

## Expression
For a solute dissolved in two different immiscible solvents that are in contact with each other, the **Partition Coefficient**, $\ce{K_{pc}}$ is given by:

$$
\large
\ce{K_{pc}} = \ce{\frac{[Solute]_{\text{Solvent A}}}{[Solute]_{{\text{Solvent B}}}}}
$$

>[!info] Units of Partition Coefficient
>Since partition coefficient is a ratio of concentrations, it has no units.


## Essential Conditions
For $\ce{K_{pc}}$ to be valid and constant:
1. **Immiscible Solvents:** The two solvents must form two distinct layers (e.g. Water and Ether).
2. **Equilibrium:** The rate of solute moving from Solvent A to B equals the rate from B to A.
3. **Constant Temperature:** Like all equilibrium constants, $\ce{K_{pc}}$ is temperature-dependent.
4. **Same Molecular State:** The solute must exist in the **same physical state** in both solvents (i.e. no dissociation or dimerization).

>[!warning] Warning: The Ratio Order
>Unlike $\ce{K_{c}}$ where it is always Products/Reactants, $\ce{K_{pc}}$ does not have a fixed universal rule for which solvent is on top, however:
>- **Cambridge Rule:** The exam question **will specify** the ratio:
>	- If it says "Calculate $\ce{K_{pc}}$ of $\ce{X}$ between water and ether", this implies that water is on top: $\ce{\frac{[Water]}{[Ether]}}$
>	- If it says "Calculate $\ce{K_{pc}}$ of $\ce{X}$ between ether and water", this implies that ether is on top: $\ce{\frac{[Ether]}{[Water]}}$
>	  
>	  
>**In general, the solvent that comes first is the one on top**


## Factors Affecting Partition Coefficient
The value of $\ce{K_{pc}}$ is determined by the relative solubility of the solute in the two solvents. 

### The Role of Polarity
1. **Polar Solutes** (e.g. Ammonia, Short-chain alcohols/acids):
	- **Prefer Polar Solvents** (e.g. Water).
	- **Reason:** Formation of **hydrogen bonds** or **strong permanent dipole-permanent dipole** forces with water.
	- $\ce{K_{pc}}$ usually favours the aqueous layer.
2. **Non-polar Solutes** (e.g. Iodine, Halogenoalkanes, long-chain organic molecules):
	- **Prefer Non-polar solvents** (e.g. Hexane, Cyclohexane, Ether).
	- **Reason:** 
		- Formation of **instantaneous dipole-induced dipole** forces with the organic solvent.
		- The energy cost to disrupt water's H-bond network is too high for non-polar solutes to overcome.
	- $\ce{K_{pc}}$ favours the organic layer.

>[!example] Example: Iodine ($\ce{I_{2}}$) between Hexane and Water
>- **Structure:** $\ce{I_{2}}$ is non-polar.
>- **Solvents:** Hexane (non-polar), Water (polar)l
>- **Observation:** $\ce{I_{2}}$ is much more soluble in hexane.
>- **$\ce{K_{pc}}$ value:** If defined as $\large\ce{\frac{[I_{2}]_{\text{hexane}}}{[I_{2}]_{{\text{water}}}}}$, $\ce{K_{pc}}$ will be very large.


## Successive Extractions
Extracting a solute form a solvent using multiple small batches is more efficient than using a single large volume at the same time.


**Scenario:**
*   You have $m$ grams of solute $X$ in $100 \text{ cm}^3$ of water.
*   $K_{pc} = \frac{[X]_{organic}}{[X]_{water}} = 4$.
*   You have $100 \text{ cm}^3$ of organic solvent available.

**Method A: Single Extraction ($1 \times 100 \text{ cm}^3$)**
Let $x$ be the grams extracted into the organic layer.
Remaining in water = $(m - x)$.

$$4 = \frac{x / 100}{(m - x) / 100}$$
$$4 = \frac{x}{m - x} \implies 4m - 4x = x \implies 5x = 4m$$
$$x = 0.8m \quad (\textbf{80\% extracted})$$

**Method B: Double Extraction ($2 \times 50 \text{ cm}^3$)**

*Extraction 1:*
Let $x_1$ be grams extracted.
$$4 = \frac{x_1 / 50}{(m - x_1) / 100}$$
$$4 = \frac{2x_1}{m - x_1} \implies 4m - 4x_1 = 2x_1 \implies 6x_1 = 4m$$
$$x_1 = 0.67m$$
*Remaining in water:* $0.33m$.

*Extraction 2:*
Now we extract from the remaining $0.33m$. Let $x_2$ be grams extracted.
$$4 = \frac{x_2 / 50}{(0.33m - x_2) / 100}$$
$$4 = \frac{2x_2}{0.33m - x_2} \implies 1.32m - 4x_2 = 2x_2 \implies 6x_2 = 1.32m$$
$$x_2 = 0.22m$$

*Total Extracted:* $0.67m + 0.22m = 0.89m$ ($\textbf{89\% extracted}$).

**Conclusion:** Method B (89%) is more efficient than Method A (80%).
