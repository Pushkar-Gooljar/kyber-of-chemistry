## Expression for Acidic Buffer
Consider an acidic buffer made using a weak acid $\ce{HA}$ and its conjugate base $\ce{A-}$, at equilibrium:
$$
\large
\ce{
HA_{(aq)} \rightleftharpoons H+_{(aq)} + A-_{(aq)}
}
$$
The acid dissociation constant ($\ce{K_{a}}$) for this equilibrium is given by:
$$
\large
\mathrm{K_{a}} = \frac{\ce{[H+][A-]}}{\ce{HA}}
$$
Rearranging the equation to make $\ce{[H+]}$ subject:
$$
\large
\ce{[H+]} = \ce{K_{a}} \times \ce{\frac{[HA]}{[A-]}}
$$
Taking logs on both sides, we get the **Henderson-Hasselbalch Equation:**
$$
\large
\begin{aligned}
\log \ce{[H+]} &= \log\left(\ce{K_{a}} \times \ce{\frac{[HA]}{[A-]}}\right) \\[10pt]
\log \ce{[H+]} &= \log \ce{K_{a}} + \log\left(\ce{\frac{[HA]}{[A-]}}\right) \\[10pt]
-\log \ce{[H+]} &= -\log \ce{K_{a}} -\log\left(\ce{\frac{[HA]}{[A-]}}\right) \\[10pt]
\mathrm{pH} &= p\mathrm{K_{a}} + \log\left(\ce{\frac{[A-]}{[HA]}}\right)
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\large
\boxed{\mathrm{pH} = p\mathrm{K_{a}} + \log\left(\ce{\frac{[Salt]}{[Acid]}}\right)}
$$
>[!danger] Critical: $\ce{[H+]}$ and $\ce{[A-]}$ are **NOT** the same
>- Recall that when finding the pH of a weak acid, we assumed that $\ce{[H+]} = \ce{[A-]}$.
>- However, this assumption is incorrect for a buffer solution.
>- Since the concentration of $\ce{A-}$ comes from the salt (e.g. Sodium Ethanoate) rather than the acid, therefore the concentration of $\ce{A-}$ is much higher than $\ce{H+}$.
