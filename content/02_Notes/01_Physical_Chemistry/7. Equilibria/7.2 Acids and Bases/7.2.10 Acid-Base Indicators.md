# How Indicators Work
An acid-base indicator is actually a **Weak Acid** itself ($\ce{HIn}$) that has a different colour from its conjugate base ($\ce{In-}$)

**The equilibrium equation is:**
$$
\large
\underset{\text{Colour A}}{\ce{HA}_{(aq)}}\phantom{a} \rightleftharpoons \phantom{a}\ce{H+}_{(aq)}\phantom{a} + \underset{\text{Colour B}}{\phantom{a}\ce{In-}_{(aq)}}
$$
## The Mechanism (Le Chatelier's Principle)
1. **In Acidic Solution (High $\ce{H+}$)**
	- The concentration of $\ce{H+}$ pushes the equilibrium to the **LEFT**.
	- The solution takes the colour of the weak acid ($\ce{HIn}$).
2. **In Alkaline Solution (Low $\ce{H+}$)**
	- The $\ce{OH-}$ from the alkaline solution reacts with the $\ce{H+}$, lowering its concentration.
	- The equilibrium shifts to the **RIGHT** to replace the $\ce{H+}$.
	- The solution takes the colour of the conjugate base ($\ce{In-}$).

# The pH Range of an Indicator
- Indicators do not change colour instantly at pH 7.
- They change colour over a specific **pH Range**, typically spanning about 2 pH units.

The pH at which the colour change occurs depends on the $\ce{pK_{In}}$ (the $\ce{pK_{a}}$ of the indicator):
$$
\large
\text{pH change} \approx \mathrm{pK_{In}}
$$

|      Indicator      | Acid Colour | Base Colour |  pH Range  |         Use Case         |
| :-----------------: | :---------: | :---------: | :--------: | :----------------------: |
|  **Methyl Orange**  |     Red     |   Yellow    | 3.1 - 4.4  |  Strong Acid titrations  |
| **Phenolphthalein** | Colourless  |    Pink     | 8.3 - 10.0 | Strong Alkali titrations |
> [!info] The Math: Why pH ≈ pKin?
> The midpoint of an indicator's color change occurs when the concentration of the Acid form ($HIn$) equals the Base form ($In^-$).
>
> Using the equilibrium expression:
> $$ K_{In} = \frac{[H^+][In^-]}{[HIn]} $$
>
> If $[HIn] = [In^-]$, they cancel out:
> $K_{In} = [H^+]$
>
> Taking negative logs of both sides gives the rule:
> $pK_{In} = \text{pH}$
>
> *This explains why different indicators work at different pH levels—it depends entirely on their specific $K_{In}$ value.*

# Selecting the Correct Indicator
To get an accurate result, the indicator must change colour **during the vertical section** of the titration curve.

**The Rule:**
The **pH Range** of the indicator must fall completely within the **Vertical Section** of the titration graph.

## Matching Indicators to titrations
### Strong Acid + Strong Alkali 

>[!info] Suitable Indicator: Both
>![pH curve of strong acid + strong alkali titration|500](https://cdn.savemyexams.com/cdn-cgi/image/f=auto,width=1920/https://cdn.savemyexams.com/uploads/2020/11/1.7-Equilibria-Strong-Acid-Strong-Alkali-Indicator.png)
>- **Vertical Section:** pH 3 $\to$ 10
>- **Suitable Indicator:** **Both**
>- **Explanation:** Both ranges (3.1-4.4 and 8.3-10.0) fall inside the huge vertical jump.

### Weak Acid + Strong Alkali
>[!info] Suitable Indicator: Phenolphthalein
>![pH curve of weak acid + strong alkali titration|500](https://cdn.savemyexams.com/cdn-cgi/image/f=auto,width=1920/https://cdn.savemyexams.com/uploads/2020/11/1.7-Equilibria-Weak-Acid-Strong-Alkali-Indicator.png)
>- **Vertical Section:** pH 7 $\to$ 10
>- **Suitable Indicator:** **Phenolphthalein**
>- **Explanation:** Vertical section is high. Methyl Orange (3.1-4.4) would change too early.


### Strong Acid + Weak Alkali
>[!info] Suitable Indicator: Methyl Orange
>![pH curve of strong acid + weak alkali titration|500](https://cdn.savemyexams.com/cdn-cgi/image/f=auto,width=1920/https://cdn.savemyexams.com/uploads/2020/11/1.7-Equilibria-Strong-Acid-Weak-Alkali-Indicator.png)
>- **Vertical Section:** pH 3 $\to$ 7
>- **Suitable Indicator:** **Methyl Orange**
>- **Explanation:** Vertical section is low. Phenolphthalein (8.3-10.0) would change too late.


### Weak Acid + Weak Alkali
>[!warning] Suitable Indicator: None
>![pH curve of weak acid + weak alkali titration|500](https://cdn.savemyexams.com/cdn-cgi/image/f=auto,width=1920/https://cdn.savemyexams.com/uploads/2020/11/1.7-Equilibria-Weak-Acid-Weak-Alkali-Indicator.png)
>- **Vertical Section:** None
>- **Suitable Indicator:** **None**
>- **Explanation:** There is no vertical section. No indicator gives a sharp endpoint.
>  
>  ***Note: A pH meter can be used instead***
