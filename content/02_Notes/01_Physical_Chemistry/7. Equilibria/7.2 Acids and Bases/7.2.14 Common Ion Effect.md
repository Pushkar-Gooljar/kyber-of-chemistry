## Definition

>[!info] Definition: Common Ion Effect
>The **Common Ion Effect** is the reduction in the solubility of a sparingly soluble salt when a soluble compound containing one of the ions of the salt is added to the solution.

## Mechanism
Consider a saturated solution of a sparingly soluble salt, such as Silver Chloride ($\ce{AgCl}$):
$$
\large
\ce{
AgCl_{(s)} \rightleftharpoons Ag+_{(aq)} + Cl-_{(aq)}
}
$$
If Sodium Chloride ($\ce{NaCl}$) is added to this solution:
1. $\ce{NaCl}$ is a strong electrolyte and dissociates completely:
$$
\large
\ce{
NaCl_{(s)} -> Na+_{(aq)} + Cl-_{(aq)}
}
$$
2. The concentration of $\ce{Cl-}$ in significantly **increases**.
3. According to **Le Chatelier's Principle**, the position of equilibrium shifts to the **left** to oppose the change (reduce concentration of $\ce{Cl-}$).
4. More $\ce{AgCl_{(s)}}$ precipitates out of solution.
5. The concentration of dissolved $\ce{Ag+_{(aq)}}$ decreases.
