# Definition of Neutralisation
>[!info] Definition: Neutralisation
>**Neutralisation** is the reaction between hydrogen ions ($\ce{H+}$) from an acid and hydroxide ions ($\ce{OH-}$) from an alkali to form water molecules.

## The Ionic Equation
For **any** reaction between a **strong acid** and a **strong alkali**, the ionic equation is identical:
$$
\large
\ce{H+_{(aq)} + OH-_{(aq)} -> H_{2}O_{(l)}}
$$
>[!tip] Thermochemistry Link
>Because the underlying reaction:
>$$\large \ce{H+_{(aq)} + OH-_{(aq)} -> H_{2}O_{(l)}}$$
>is the same for all strong acid/alkali pairs, the **Enthalpy Change of Neutralisation** ($\mathrm{\Delta H_{neut}}$) is roughly constant at:
>$$\large -57 \space \mathrm{kJmol^{-1}}$$

#  Formation of Salts
A **Salt** is a chemical compound formed when the hydrogen ions ($\ce{H+}$) in an acid is replaced by a metal ion (or ammonium ion, $\ce{NH_{4}+}$).
