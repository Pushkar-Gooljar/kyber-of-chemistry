## What is Solubility?
In A Level chemistry, we deal with "sparingly soluble" salts (salts that barely dissolve, like Silver Chloride or Barium Sulfate). Even though they look insoluble, a tiny amount *does* dissolve to form a saturated solution.

When an **undissolved ionic compound** is in contact with a **saturated solution** of its ions, an equilibrium is established:
$$
\large
\ce{MgCl_{2}_{(s)} \rightleftharpoons Mg^2+_{(aq)} +2Cl-_{(aq)}}
$$

## Solubility Product
Consider the equilibrium when the sparingly soluble salt $\ce{MgCl_{2}}$ is added to water:
$$
\large
\ce{MgCl_{2}_{(s)} \rightleftharpoons Mg^2+_{(aq)} +2Cl-_{(aq)}}
$$
The equilibrium constant ($\ce{K_{c}}$) for this equilibrium is given by:
$$
\large
\ce{K_{c} = \frac{\ce{[Mg^2+_{(aq)}][Cl-_{(aq)}]^2}}{\ce{[MgCl_{2}_{(s)}]}}}
$$
Since the concentration of the solid $\ce{MgCl_{2}}$ is constant (constant density), we can multiply both sides by it to get an expression for the **solubility constant**, $\ce{K_{sp}}$:
$$
\large
\ce{K_{sp}} = \ce{[Mg^2+_{(aq)}][Cl-_{(aq)}]^2}
$$
>[!info] Definition: Solubility Product ($\ce{K_{sp}}$)
>The **Solubility Product ($\ce{K_{sp}}$)** is:
>- The product of the concentrations of each ion in a saturated solution of a sparingly soluble salt
>- At 298 K
>- Raised to the power of their relative concentrations.


---
## Ionic Product
The **Ionic Product ($Q$)** is calculated using the exact same formula as $\ce{K_{sp}}$, but using the **actual concentrations** of ions at a specific moment in time, not necessarily at equilibrium.
$$
\large
Q = \ce{[A^x+]^m[A^y+]^n}
$$
>[!important]
>- $\ce{K_{sp}}$: Describes the **maximum** possible concentration (Saturation limit).
>- $Q$: Describes the **current** concentration.

---
## Predicting Precipitation 
By comparing the value of $Q$ to the fixed value of $\ce{K_{sp}}$, we can predict whether a solid will form.

| Condition         | State of Solution  | Physical Result                                                                                              |
| ----------------- | ------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| $Q < \ce{K_{sp}}$ | **Unsaturated**    | No precipitate forms.<br>More solid can dissolve.                                                            |
| $Q = \ce{K_{sp}}$ | **Saturated**      | No precipitate forms, but no more solid can dissolve.<br>Equilibrium is formed.                              |
| $Q > \ce{K_{sp}}$ | **Supersaturated** | Precipitate forms.<br>The excess ions crash out of solution until $Q$ drops back down to equal $\ce{K_{sp}}$ |
