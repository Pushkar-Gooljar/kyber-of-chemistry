## 1. The Definition
- The Brønsted-Lowry theory implies that acid-base reactions are **reversible**. 
- When an acid acts as a proton donor, it forms a new species that has the potential to accept that proton back.

>[!info] Conjugate Acid-Base Pair
>A pair of species that differ by exactly **one proton** ($\ce{H+}$).
>- The acid has the proton.
>- The base lacks the proton.


## 2. The General Equation  


![[excalidraw_conjugate_acid_base.svg|400]]
- **Pair 1:** $\ce{HA}$ and $\ce{A-}$
- **Pair 2:** $\ce{B}$ and $\ce{BH+}$


>[!summary] 
>- $\large\ce{Acid \underset{-H+}{\to} Conj Base}$
>- $\large\ce{Base \underset{+H+}{\to} Conj Acid}$

## 3. Examples 
### Example 1: Methanoic Acid + Water
$$ \ce{HCOOH(aq) + H2O(l) \rightleftharpoons HCOO^-(aq) + H3O^+(aq)} $$
*   **Acid:** $$\ce{HCOOH}$$ (Donates H) $\rightarrow$ **Conj Base 1:** $$\ce{HCOO^-}$$
*   **Base:** $$\ce{H2O}$$ (Accepts H) $\rightarrow$ **Conj Acid 2:** $$\ce{H3O^+}$$

### Example 2: Ammonia + Water
$$ \ce{NH3(aq) + H2O(l) \rightleftharpoons NH4^+(aq) + OH^-(aq)} $$
*   **Base:** $$\ce{NH3}$$ (Accepts H) $\rightarrow$ **Conj Acid 1:** $$\ce{NH4^+}$$
*   **Acid:** $$\ce{H2O}$$ (Donates H) $\rightarrow$ **Conj Base 2:** $$\ce{OH^-}$$
## 4. The Inverse Relationship of Strength 
>[!tip] The "See-Saw" Rule
>- The **Stronger** the Acid $\to$ The **Weaker** its Conjugate Base.
>- The **Weaker** the Acid $\to$ the **Stronger** its Conjugate Base.

### Why does this matter?
- It explains why reactions proceed in a certain direction.
- Equilibrium always favours the formation of the **weaker** acid and base (the more stable species).


**Case A: Strong Acid ($\ce{HCl}$**)
$$
\large\ce{HCl -> H+ + Cl-}
$$
- Since $\ce{HCl}$ is a very **strong** acid (desperate to donate proton), $\ce{Cl-}$ is an incredibly **weak** base (has zero desire to accept the proton back).
- Result: The reaction goes to completion.

**Case B: Weak Acid (Ethanoic Acid)**
$$
\large
\ce{CH_{3}COOH \rightleftharpoons H+ + CH_{3}COO-}
$$
- Since $\ce{CH_{3}COOH}$ is a **weak** acid, $\ce{CH_{3}COO-}$ is a reasonably **strong** conjugate base.
- The $\ce{CH_{3}COO-}$ actively attacks protons to reform the acid.
- Result: The reaction is reversible and equilibrium lies to the left.

## 5. Amphoteric Nature of Water
Notice in the examples above that water can be part of two different pairs depending on what it reacts with.

### 1. Water as a Base
$$
\large
\ce{HCl + H_{2}O -> Cl- + H_{3}O+}  
$$
- Conjugate Acid of water is **Hydronium** ($\ce{H_{3}O+}$).

### 2. Water as an Acid
$$
\large
\ce{NH_{3} + H_{2}O \rightleftharpoons NH_{4}+ + OH-}
$$
- Conjugate Base of water is **Hydroxide** ($\ce{OH-}$).
