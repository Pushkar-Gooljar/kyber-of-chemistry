>[!info] Acid and Base Definitions
>- **Acid:** A proton ($\ce{H+}$) **donor**.
>- **Base:** A proton ($\ce{H+}$) **acceptor**.

>[!tip] Examiner's Tip
>In exams, always use the word "**proton**" rather than "hydrogen ion". It shows a higher level of theoretical understanding.

## The Mechanism
Acids and bases do not exist in isolation. For something to act as an acid (donate $\ce{H+}$), something else must be there to act as a base (accept $\ce{H+}$).

### Example 1: Dissolving Acid in Water
$$\large \ce{\underset{Acid}{HCl} + \underset{Base}{H2O} \to H3O^+ + Cl^-} $$
- $\ce{HCl}$ **donates** a proton to water.
- $\ce{Water}$ **accepts** the proton (acting as a base).
### Example 2: Reaction between Ammonia and HCl
$$ \large\ce{\underset{Base}{NH3} + \underset{Acid}{HCl} \to NH4^+ + Cl^-} $$
- $\ce{HCl}$ **donates** a proton
- $\ce{NH_{3}}$ **accepts** the proton using the one pair on the nitrogen atom.
