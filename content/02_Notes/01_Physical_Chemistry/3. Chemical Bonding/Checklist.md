# 📝 9701 AS Chemistry Checklist: 3. Chemical Bonding

> [!info] How to use this checklist
> As an expert examiner, I can tell you that the difference between a B and an A* in this chapter lies in **precision of language**. Knowing a concept isn't enough; you must write the exact keywords the mark scheme demands. Pay special attention to the **Examiner Tips**—these are direct observations of where thousands of candidates have lost marks in past papers.

## 3.1 & 3.6 Electronegativity & Bond Polarity
- [ ] **Define Electronegativity:** I can define it exactly as "the power of an atom to attract electrons to itself (in a covalent bond)."
- [ ] **Explain Trends:** I can explain why electronegativity increases across a period (increasing nuclear charge, similar shielding) and decreases down a group (increasing atomic radius/shielding).
- [ ] **Define Bond Polarity:** I can describe it as the asymmetric distribution of electron density between two bonded atoms due to a difference in electronegativity.
- [ ] **Predict Bond Types:** I can use Pauling electronegativity values to predict whether a bond will be non-polar covalent, polar covalent, or ionic.
- [ ] **Dipole Moments:** I can determine if a whole molecule has an overall dipole moment based on its shape (e.g., $CO_2$ has no overall dipole because individual dipoles cancel out, but $SO_2$ does).

## 3.2 Ionic Bonding
- [ ] **Definition:** I can define ionic bonding as the "electrostatic attraction between oppositely charged ions."
- [ ] **Dot-and-Cross Diagrams:** I can draw accurate dot-and-cross diagrams for ionic compounds (e.g., $NaCl, MgO, CaF_2, LiH$).
	- [ ] *Examiner Tip:* Always include square brackets around the ions and place the charge outside the bracket. Remember that the metal ion usually has an empty outer shell in the diagram.
- [ ] **Physical Properties:** I can explain why ionic compounds have high melting points (strong electrostatic attraction requires a lot of energy to overcome).
- [ ] **Conductivity:** I can explain that ionic compounds conduct electricity only when molten or aqueous because the **ions are mobile/free to move**. 
	- [ ] *Examiner Tip:* NEVER use the phrase "delocalised electrons" or "free electrons" when discussing ionic conductivity. You will lose the mark instantly.

## 3.3 Metallic Bonding
- [ ] **Definition:** I can define metallic bonding as the "electrostatic attraction between positive metal ions and delocalised electrons."
- [ ] **Diagrammatic Representation:** I can draw a labelled diagram of a metallic lattice.
	- [ ] *Examiner Tip:* You must draw at least two rows of positive ions. Label the circles as "positive metal ions/cations" (NOT nuclei or protons) and the spaces between as "delocalised electrons."
- [ ] **Melting Point Trends:** I can explain why melting points increase from $Na$ to $Al$ (greater nuclear charge, smaller ionic radius, and more delocalised electrons per atom lead to stronger electrostatic attraction).
- [ ] **Conductivity:** I can explain that metals conduct electricity because delocalised electrons are mobile.

## 3.4 Covalent and Coordinate (Dative) Bonding
- [ ] **Definition:** I can define a covalent bond as the "electrostatic attraction between the nuclei of two atoms and a shared pair of electrons."
- [ ] **Sigma ($\sigma$) vs Pi ($\pi$) Bonds:**
	- [ ] I can describe a $\sigma$ bond as the **direct/head-on** overlap of orbitals.
	- [ ] I can describe a $\pi$ bond as the **sideways** overlap of adjacent p-orbitals (above and below the plane).
	- [ ] I can draw diagrams showing the formation of both $\sigma$ and $\pi$ bonds.
	- [ ] *Examiner Tip:* In addition reactions, the $\pi$ bond breaks because the electrons are further from the nuclei and experience weaker electrostatic attraction, making them more susceptible to electrophilic attack.
- [ ] **Coordinate (Dative) Bonding:**
	- [ ] I can define a coordinate bond as a covalent bond where both electrons in the shared pair come from the same atom.
	- [ ] I can draw structures showing dative bonds with an arrow pointing from the donor to the acceptor (e.g., $NH_4^+, Al_2Cl_6, CO$).
- [ ] **Bond Energy and Reactivity:** I can relate bond energy to reactivity (e.g., $N_2$ is highly unreactive because of its very high bond energy/strong $N \equiv N$ triple bond; $P_4$ is more reactive than $N_2$ because $P-P$ bonds are weaker).
- [ ] **Octet Expansion:** I can explain that Period 3 elements (like P, S, Cl) can expand their octet because they have accessible 3d-orbitals.

## 3.5 Shapes of Molecules & VSEPR Theory
- [ ] **VSEPR Principle:** I can state that electron pairs repel each other to be as far apart as possible, and the order of repulsion is: Lone Pair-Lone Pair > Lone Pair-Bonding Pair > Bonding Pair-Bonding Pair.
- [ ] I have memorised the exact bond angles and names of the following standard shapes:
	- [ ] Linear ($180^\circ$) e.g., $CO_2$
	- [ ] Trigonal Planar ($120^\circ$) e.g., $BF_3, SO_3$
	- [ ] Tetrahedral ($109.5^\circ$) e.g., $CH_4, NH_4^+, PCl_4^+$
	- [ ] Trigonal Bipyramidal ($120^\circ$ and $90^\circ$) e.g., $PF_5, PCl_5$
	- [ ] Octahedral ($90^\circ$) e.g., $SF_6, TeF_6, PCl_6^-$
- [ ] I can deduce the shape and angle of derivatives with lone pairs:
	- [ ] Pyramidal ($107^\circ$) e.g., $NH_3, NI_3$ (1 lone pair, 3 bonding pairs)
	- [ ] Non-linear / V-shaped / Bent ($104.5^\circ$) e.g., $H_2O, SCl_2$ (2 lone pairs, 2 bonding pairs)
- [ ] **Hybridisation:** I can correctly identify the hybridisation of atoms in organic and inorganic molecules:
	- [ ] $sp^3$: 4 sigma bonds (e.g., alkanes)
	- [ ] $sp^2$: 3 sigma bonds, 1 pi bond (e.g., alkenes, carbonyls)
	- [ ] $sp$: 2 sigma bonds, 2 pi bonds (e.g., alkynes, nitriles, $N_2$)

## 3.6 Intermolecular Forces (IMFs) & Physical Properties
> [!warning] Examiner Tip: Melting and Boiling Points
> The most common error in Paper 2 is confusing what breaks when a substance melts. 
> - Melting **Giant Covalent** structures ($SiO_2, \text{diamond}$): Breaks **strong covalent bonds**.
> - Melting **Simple Molecular** structures ($P_4, S_8, Cl_2, I_2, CO_2, P_4O_{10}$): Breaks **WEAK intermolecular forces (London forces, pd-pd, or H-bonds)**, *NOT* covalent bonds.

- [ ] **Instantaneous Dipole - Induced Dipole (London/Dispersion) Forces:**
	- [ ] I can explain that id-id forces increase in strength as the **number of electrons** in the molecule increases.
	- [ ] I can use this to explain trends (e.g., boiling point of halogens: $I_2 > Br_2 > Cl_2$; or Group 16 hydrides $H_2Te > H_2Se > H_2S$; or $S_8 > P_4$ because $S_8$ has more electrons).
- [ ] **Permanent Dipole - Permanent Dipole (pd-pd) Forces:**
	- [ ] I can identify molecules that possess pd-pd forces (asymmetrical polar molecules).
- [ ] **Hydrogen Bonding:**
	- [ ] I know the strict criteria: a Hydrogen atom covalently bonded to a highly electronegative atom (N, O, or F) interacting with a lone pair on another N, O, or F.
	- [ ] **Drawing H-bonds:** I can draw a perfect H-bond between two molecules (e.g., $NH_3, H_2O$, Lactic Acid). 
	- [ ] *Examiner Tip for Drawings:* You MUST include (1) Relevant lone pairs, (2) all relevant partial charges ($\delta+$ and $\delta-$), and (3) a dashed line showing the H-bond originating *directly from the lone pair* to the $\delta+ H$.
- [ ] **Anomalous Properties of Water:**
	- [ ] I can explain why ice is less dense than water (H-bonds hold molecules in an open lattice structure with more empty space).
	- [ ] I can explain its relatively high melting/boiling point and surface tension compared to other Group 16 hydrides (due to strong hydrogen bonds requiring significant energy to overcome).
- [ ] I can compare the thermal stability of Hydrogen Halides ($HF$ to $HI$) and explain that stability *decreases* down the group because the **atomic radius of the halogen increases**, making the $H-X$ bond longer and weaker (less orbital overlap). *Note: Thermal stability relies on covalent bond strength, NOT intermolecular forces!*

## 3.7 Master List of Highly-Tested Dot-and-Cross Diagrams
*I have practiced drawing dot-and-cross diagrams (showing outer electrons only) for the following exact molecules that have appeared in past papers:*
- [ ] $N_2$ (Triple bond, one lone pair on each N)
- [ ] $CO$ (Triple bond consisting of 2 normal covalent and 1 dative bond from O to C. Lone pair on C and O).
- [ ] $CS_2$ (Linear, double bonds, similar to $CO_2$)
- [ ] $SCl_2$ (V-shaped, 2 bonding pairs, 2 lone pairs on S, full octets on Cl)
- [ ] $POCl_3$ (Expanded octet on P: double bond to O, single bonds to 3 Cls. 10 electrons around P)
- [ ] $Al_2Cl_6$ (Dimer: Two $AlCl_3$ molecules joined by two dative bonds from the Cl lone pairs of one molecule to the empty orbital of the Al on the other)
- [ ] $NH_4Cl$ (Must show the covalent/dative bonds in $NH_4^+$ inside a bracket with a $[+] $ charge, and $Cl$ in a separate bracket with 8 crosses/dots and a $[-]$ charge).
- [ ] $N_2F_2$ (Double bond between N=N, single bonds to F. One lone pair on each N).
- [ ] $SO_2$ (Can be drawn with one double bond and one dative bond, OR two double bonds with an expanded octet. Both are accepted, but ensure S has a lone pair).
