# The Oxidation Number Method
>[!concept] 
>The **Oxidation Number Method** is a systematic approach to balancing redox equations. It relies on the principle of conservation of charge: The total increase in oxidation number must equal the total decrease in oxidation number.

---
# Step-by-Step Guide
>[!example] 
>Write a balanced ionic equation for the reaction of $\ce{K_{2}Cr_{2}O_{7}}$ with $\ce{NaI}$
>

## Step 1
>[!info] Identify the Key Species
>- Identify the reactants (oxidising and reducing agents) and their products.
>- Only write down the ions involved and not the spectator ions.

$$
\ce{
Cr_{2}O_{7}^2- + I- -> Cr^3+ + I_{2}
}
$$
## Step 2
>[!info] Balance Number of Atoms
>- Balance the number of atoms for the O.A. and R.A. and their products.

$$
\ce{
Cr_{2}O_{7}^2- + 2I- -> 2Cr^3+ + I_{2}
}
$$
## Step 3
>[!info] ON and TON
>- Calculate the **Oxidation Number (ON)** for each species
>- Calculate the **Total Oxidation Number (TON)** for each species by multiplying the Oxidation Number with the stoichiometric constant (number in front of species).

![‎|500](on-ton-cr.svg)

## Step 4
>[!info] Change in TON
>- Calculate the change in TON for the O.A. and R.A.
>- Simplify by dividing by a common factor if possible.

![‎‎|500](change-ton.svg)

![‎‎|500](divide-ton-change.svg)


## Step 5
>[!info] Cross-Multiply by Change in TON
>- Multiply the O.A and its product by the change in TON of the R.A
>- Multiply the R.A and its product by the change in TON of the O.A
>- Simplify the stoichiometric constants

![‎‎|500](ton-cross-multiply.svg)
$$
\ce{
Cr_{2}O_{7}^2- + 6I- -> 2Cr^3+
 + 3I_{2}}
$$
# Step 6
>[!info] Balancing Oxygen
>- Add $\ce{H_{2}O}$ to balance number of oxygen atoms

$$
\ce{
Cr_{2}O_{7}^2- + 6I- -> 2Cr^3 + 3I_{2} + 7H_{2}O
}
$$
## Step 7
>[!info] Balancing Hydrogen
>- Add $\ce{H+}$ to balance number of hydrogen atoms

$$
\ce{
Cr_{2}O_{7}^2- + 6I- + 14H+-> 2Cr^3 + 3I_{2} + 7H_{2}O
}
$$
## Step 8: If in Alkaline Medium
>[!warning] Neutralising $\ce{H+}$
>**Only do this step if the reaction occurs in alkaline medium**
>1. Carry out **Step 7** as usual
>2. Add $\ce{OH-}$ on both sides to cancel out $\ce{H+}$, as such, number of $\ce{OH-}$ added to each side should be equal to number of $\ce{H+}$ present.
>3. Combine the $\ce{H+}$ and $\ce{OH-}$ to form water.
>4. Simplify by cancelling out excess $\ce{H_{2}O}$ molecules present on both sides of the equation.

## Step 9: State Symbols
>[!warning] Adding State Symbols
>**Only add state symbols if asked to, if you add when not asked and make a mistake, your answer may pass for CON (contradictory) and lose marks**

$$
\ce{
Cr_{2}O_{7}^2-(aq) + 6I-(aq) + 14H+ (aq)-> 2Cr^3(aq) + 3I_{2}(aq) + 7H_{2}O(l)
}
$$

# Disproportionation Reactions
>[!info] Disproportionation
>A disproportionation reaction is one in which the same species is oxidised and reduced.

>[!important] Oxidation Number Method for Disproportionation
>Lets say you have the species $X$ disproportionating to give $A$ and $B$:
>$$\ce{X -> A + B}$$
>Write $X$ two times in the reactants side:
>$$\ce{X + X -> A + B}$$
>Use the usual Oxidation Number Method, and at the end just combine the $X$'s together.
