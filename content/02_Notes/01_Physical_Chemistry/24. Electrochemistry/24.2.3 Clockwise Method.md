# The Clockwise Method for Standard Electrode Potentials

The **Clockwise Method** is a reliable visual mnemonic used to quickly determine the spontaneous direction of redox half-reactions, identify which species is oxidized/reduced, and assign the cathode and anode in an electrochemical cell.

---

## 🧭 The Core Principle

To find the spontaneous direction of reactions between two redox couples:
1. Write both standard reduction half-equations.
2. Arrange them vertically with the **more positive $\mathrm{E^{⦵}}$ value on top** and the **more negative $\mathrm{E^{⦵}}$ value on the bottom**.
3. Trace a **clockwise loop** starting from the top-left reactant.

![Clockwise Method|600](clockwise-method-redox.svg)

>[!concept] Interpreting This Diagram
>In this case:
>
>**1. At the Cathode**
>$$\ce{Ag+ + e- -> Ag}$$
>
>**2. At the Anode**
>$$\ce{Zn -> Zn^2+ + 2e-}$$
>
>**3. $\mathrm{E^{⦵}_{\text{cell}}}$**
>$$\mathrm{E^{⦵}_{\text{cell}}} = +0.80 - (-0.76) = +1.56 \text{ V}$$

## 💡 Summary Table

| Position | $E^\theta$ Characteristic | Electrode | Process | Direction of Half-Reaction |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Top** | More Positive | **Cathode** | Reduction | Forward ($\text{Reactants} \rightarrow \text{Products}$) |
| **Bottom** | More Negative | **Anode** | Oxidation | Reverse ($\text{Products} \rightarrow \text{Reactants}$) |
