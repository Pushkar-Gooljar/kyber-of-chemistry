# Electrode Potential ($\mathrm{E}$)
>[!important] $\mathrm{E}$ vs $\mathrm{E^{⦵}}$
>Standard Electrode Potentials ($\mathrm{E^{⦵}}$) are only valid under **standard conditions**:
>1. Temperature of $298\text{ K}$
>2. Pressure of $101\text{ kPa}$
>3. Concentration of $1.00\text{ mol dm}^{-3}$ for all aqueous ions.
>   
>If you change any of these conditions, you are now operating under **non-standard conditions**. Under non-standard conditions, we drop the superscript ${⦵}$ and use the symbol $\mathrm{E}$ instead of $\mathrm{E^{⦵}}$.

# Le Chatelier's Principle

>[!concept] Standard Electrode Potential as an Equilibrium
>Every Standard Electrode Potential half-equation is written as a **reduction equilibrium** (the reactants get reduced):
>$$\text{Reactants} + ne^- \rightleftharpoons \text{Products}$$
>
>To determine how concentration changes affect the electrode potential $\mathrm{E}$, you simply apply **Le Chatelier's Principle**

>[!hint]
>- The higher the oxidising power becomes, the more positive $\mathrm{E}$ becomes and the lower the oxidising power becomes, the more negative $\mathrm{E}$ becomes.
>- Oxidising power increases if forward reaction is favoured.
>- Oxidising power decreases if backward reaction is favoured.

## Changing Concentration of Species on the Left
>[!example] Increasing Concentration of Species on Left
>- According to LCP, the equilibrium shifts to the **right** to minimise the effect of the change.
>- This favours the forward reaction (**reduction**).
>- The species becomes **more easily reduced** (higher oxidising power).
>
>**Result:** The electrode potential ($\mathrm{E}$) becomes **more positive**.

>[!example] Decreasing Concentration of Species on Left
>- According to LCP, the equilibrium shifts to the **left** to minimise the effect of the change.
>- this favours the backward reaction (**oxidation**).
>- The species becomes **harder to reduce** (lower oxidising power).
>
>**Result:** The electrode potential ($\mathrm{E}$) becomes **more negative**.

## Changing Concentration of Species on the Right
>[!example] Increasing Concentration of Species on Left

>
>**Result:** The electrode potential ($\mathrm{E}$) becomes **more negative**.

>[!example] Decreasing Concentration of Species on Left
>- According to LCP, the equilibrium shifts to the **right** to minimise the effect of the change.
