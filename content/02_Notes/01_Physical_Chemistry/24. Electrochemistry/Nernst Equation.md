# The Nernst Equation
>[!info] Nernst Equation
>The **Nernst Equation** is the mathematical tool used to calculate the actual electrode potential ($\mathrm{E}$) of a half-cell or overall cell potential ($\mathrm{E_{\text{cell}}}$) under non standard conditions.

## General Nernst Equation
$$
\mathrm{E} = \mathrm{E^{⦵}} + \frac{RT}{zF}  \ln\left( \frac{\text{[oxidised species]}}{\text{[reduced species]}} \right)
$$
**Where**,
- $\mathrm{E}$ = Electrode potential under non-standard conditions (V)
- $\mathrm{E^{⦵}}$ = Standard Electrode Potential (V).
- $R$ = Ideal gas constant ($8.31\text{ K}^{-1}\text{ mol}^{-1}$).
- $T$ = Thermodynamic temperature (K)
- $z$ = The number of moles of electrons transferred in the half-reaction.
- $F$ = Faraday's constant ($96500\text{ C mol}^{-1}$)

## Simplified Nernst Equation ($298\text{ K}$)
>[!info] Simplified Nernst Equation
>The syllabus explicitly mentions knowledge of the simplified Nernst Equation, in which the thermodynamic temperature $T$ is taken to be constant at room temperature ($298\text{ K}$) and the $\ln$ is replaced by $\log_{10}$:
>
>$$\mathrm{E}= \mathrm{E^{⦵}} + \frac{0.059}{z}\lg\left( \frac{\text{[oxidised species]}}{\text{[reduced species]}} \right)$$
>

> [!math]- Derivation of Simplified Nernst Equation
> Using laws of logarithms:
> $$
> \ln(x) = 2.303 \lg(x)
> $$
> Substituting this conversion and $T=298$ and $F=96500$ in the general Nernst Equation:
> $$
> \begin{align}
> \mathrm{E}&=\mathrm{E^{⦵}}+\frac{{8.31 \times 298}}{z \times 96500} \times 2.303 \lg\left( \frac{\text{[oxidised species]}}{\text{[reduced species]}} \right) \\
> \mathrm{E}&= \mathrm{E^{⦵}} + \frac{0.059}{z}\lg\left( \frac{\text{[oxidised species]}}{\text{[reduced species]}} \right)
> \end{align}
> $$


## Concentration of Solids and Gases
>[!important] Concentration of Solids and Gases
>The concentration of pure solids, pure liquids, and standard gases is defined as constant (1)
>
>**Therefore the concentrations of solids and gases are set to $1.00\text{ mol dm}^{-3}$.**
