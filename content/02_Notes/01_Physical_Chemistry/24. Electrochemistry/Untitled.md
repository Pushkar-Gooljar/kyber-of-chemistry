# Standard Electrode and Cell Potentials
>[!info] Standard Electrode Potential ($\ce{E^{⦵}}$)
>- The voltage of a half-cell compared to a **standard hydrogen electrode**
>- Measured under standard conditions of $298 \space \mathrm{K}$, $101 \space \mathrm{kPa}$ pressure, and $1\text{ mol dm}^{-3}$ ion concentration

>[!info] Standard Cell Potential ($\mathrm{E^{⦵}_{cell}}$)
>- The potential difference between two half-cells
>- Measured under standard conditions of $298 \space \mathrm{K}$, $101 \space \mathrm{kPa}$ pressure, and $1\text{ mol dm}^{-3}$ ion concentration

>[!important] Standard Conditions 
>Standard Electrode Potential ($\ce{E^{⦵}}$) and Standard Cell Potential ($\mathrm{E^{⦵}_{cell}}$) are always measured under standard conditions.
>
>**Standard Conditions:**
>- Temperature of $298 \text{{ K}}$
>- Pressure of $101 \text{ kPa}$
>- Ion concentration of $1.00 \text{ mol dm}^{-3}$


## Standard Hydrogen Electrode (SHE)
>[!info] Standard Hydrogen Electrode (SHE)
>- The **standard hydrogen electrode** is a half-cell used as a **reference electrode**.
>- It consists of hydrogen gas ($\ce{H_{2}}$) at $101 \text{ kPa}$ in equilibrium with $\ce{H+}$ ions of concentration $1.00 \text{ mol dm}^{-3}$:
>$$\ce{2H+(aq) + 2e- \rightleftharpoons H_{2}(g)}$$
>- It is arbitrarily assigned a **Standard Electrode Potential ($\mathrm{E^{⦵}}$)** of $0.00 \text{ V}$.
>

>[!important] Why is a Platinum ($\ce{Pt}$) electrode used?
>- Platinum is inert, conducts electricity, and provides a surface area for the redox equilibrium to occur without taking part in the reaction itself.

>[!concept] Use of a Standard Hydrogen Electrode
>- Because we cannot directly measure the potential of a single half-cell, the **Standard Hydrogen Electrode** is connected to it to be used as a reference.
>- Since the **Standard Hydrogen Electrode** has a $\mathrm{E^{⦵}}$ of $0.00 \text{ V}$, a voltmeter connected to the SHE and the half-cell to be measured will simply give the $\mathrm{E^{⦵}}$ of the half-cell.
>
