## 2. Mechanism: $S_{E}Ar$
>[!mechanism] Alkylation: Electrophilic Substitution 
>![](3b798.svg)
>- The carbocation is attracted to the delocalised $\pi$ bond of benzene. 
>- It eventually breaks the ring of electrons and forms a dative $\sigma$ bond to one of the carbon atoms of the ring.
>- The remaining four $\pi$ electrons are spread over the remaining five carbon atoms of the ring, in a five-centre delocalised orbital, forming an intermediate carbocation.
>- The carbocation loses a proton, the electrons of the former $\ce{C-H}$ bond reform the sextet of $\pi$ electrons.




## 3. Formation of Electrophile
>[!mechanism] Formation of alkyl carbocation
> ![](2b0c0.svg)
> - The aluminium atom in anhydrous aluminium chloride is electron-deficient, making it a Lewis Acid.
> - The aluminium atom accepts a lone pair of electron from one of the chlorine atoms to form a dative bond.
> - This highly polarises the $\ce{RCO-Cl}$ bond, weakening it.
> - Eventually, the $\ce{RCO-Cl}$  bond breaks by **heterolytic fission**.
> - The products are the strong electrophile $\ce{CH_{3}C+=O}$ and $\ce{[AlCl_{4}]-}$

## 4. Regeneration of Catalyst
>[!mechanism] Regeneration of Catalyst
>$$\large\ce{H+ + [AlCl_{4}]- -> HCl + AlCl_{3}}$$
>- The $\ce{H+}$ formed by the $S_{E}Ar$ mechanism reacts with the $\ce{[AlCl_{4}]-}$ formed during the formation of the electrophile.
>- The halogen carrier catalyst ($\ce{AlCl_{3}}$) is regenerated.
