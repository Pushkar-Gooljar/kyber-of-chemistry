## 2. Mechanism: $S_{E}Ar$
>[!mechanism] Bromination: Electrophilic Substitution 
>![](21a8a.svg)
>![](2eafa.svg)
>- The bromine cation is attracted to the delocalised $\pi$ bond of benzene. 
>- It eventually breaks the ring of electrons and forms a dative $\sigma$ bond to one of the carbon atoms of the ring.
>- The remaining four $\pi$ electrons are spread over the remaining five carbon atoms of the ring, in a five-centre delocalised orbital, forming an intermediate carbocation.
>- The carbocation loses a proton, the electrons of the former $\ce{C-H}$ bond reform the sextet of $\pi$ electrons.



## 3. Formation of Electrophile

>[!info] Halogen Carrier Catalysts
>A **halogen carrier catalyst** is a **Lewis acid** that helps generate the strong electrophile needed to react with the highly stable benzene ring.
>
>**Examples of halogen carrier catalysts:**
>- **For chlorination:** $\ce{AlCl_{3}}$ or $\ce{FeCl_{3}}$
>- **For bromination:** $\ce{AlBr_{3}}$ or $\ce{FeBr_{3}}$
>
>‎
>>[!tip]- Need for a Halogen Carrier Catalyst
>>- The continuous ring of delocalised $\pi$-electrons present in benzene make it exceptionally stable.
>>- Alkenes easily react with diatomic halogens because the double bond is reactive enough to polarise the halogen molecule on its own.
>>- Benzene is too stable to do this.
>>- Therefore a catalyst is needed to "activate" the halogen molecule and turn it into a powerful electrophile capable of breaking benzene's aromatic stability.

>[!mechanism] Formation of Electrophile 
> ![](7daf1.svg)
> - The iron atom in anhydrous iron (II) bromide is electron-deficient, making it a Lewis Acid.
> - The iron atom accepts a lone pair of electron from one of the bromine atoms to form a dative bond.
> - This highly polarises the $\ce{Br-Br}$ bond, weakening it.
> - Eventually, the $\ce{Br-Br}$ bond breaks by **heterolytic fission**.
> - The products are the strong electrophile $\ce{Br+}$ and $\ce{[FeBr_{4}]-}$


## 4. Regeneration of Catalyst
>[!mechanism] Regeneration of Catalyst
>$$\large\ce{H+ + [FeBr_{4}]- -> HBr + FeBr_{3}}$$
>- The $\ce{H+}$ formed by the $S_{E}Ar$ mechanism reacts with the $\ce{[FeBr_{4}]-}$ formed during the formation of the electrophile.
>- The halogen carrier catalyst ($\ce{FeBr_{3}}$) is regenerated.
