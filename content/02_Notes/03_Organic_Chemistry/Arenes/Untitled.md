# Note 1: ⌬ Aromatic Stability & The Preference for Substitution
**Tags:** #Chemistry/A2 #Organic/Arenes #Aromaticity 

### The Nature of the Benzene Ring
Benzene ($C_6H_6$) consists of six carbon atoms arranged in a regular planar hexagon. 
*   Every carbon atom is **sp² hybridised**, forming three $\sigma$ (sigma) bonds (bond angle $120^\circ$).
*   The remaining non-hybridised p-orbitals (one on each carbon) overlap sideways to form a **delocalised $\pi$-electron system** (a continuous ring of electron density above and below the planar carbon skeleton).

((page 2))
((page 3))

> [!check] Mark Scheme Essential
> When asked to describe the bonding in benzene, you *must* use the phrases: **"planar"**, **"p-orbitals overlap sideways"**, and **"delocalised $\pi$-electron system"**. All C-C bonds are identical in length (0.139 nm), intermediate between a single and double bond. ((page 6))

### Why Substitution over Addition?
Alkenes undergo *electrophilic addition*, but benzene strongly prefers **electrophilic substitution**. Why?

1.  **Aromatic Stability (Delocalisation Energy):** The delocalised $\pi$-system confers exceptional thermodynamic stability to the benzene ring.
2.  **Addition destroys aromaticity:** An addition reaction would permanently break the continuous $\pi$-system, resulting in a significantly less stable, non-aromatic product. 
3.  **Substitution preserves aromaticity:** In substitution, a hydrogen atom is replaced by an electrophile. The $\pi$-system is only *temporarily* disrupted in the intermediate stage, but is fully restored in the final product.

> [!deep dive] The Thermodynamic Argument
> Benzene is roughly $152 \text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$ more stable than the theoretical hypothetical "cyclohexatriene" (Kekulé structure). An addition reaction would require overcoming this massive stabilization energy, which is why benzene does not decolourise bromine water at room temperature! ((page 16))

# Note 2: ⚙️ Mechanism: Electrophilic Substitution ($S_EAr$)
**Tags:** #Chemistry/A2 #Organic/Mechanisms #Arenes

Regardless of the specific reaction (nitration, halogenation, Friedel-Crafts), the core mechanism for benzene remains strictly identical. The high electron density of the $\pi$-ring attracts powerful **electrophiles ($E^+$)**.

### The 2-Step Mechanism

**Step 1: Attack of the Electrophile (Rate-Determining Step)**
*   Two $\pi$-electrons from the delocalised ring are used to form a dative covalent bond with the electrophile ($E^+$).
*   This breaks the aromatic ring, forming an unstable, non-aromatic **intermediate carbocation** (often called the Wheland intermediate).
*   *Drawing rule:* The "horseshoe" representing the remaining delocalised electrons in the intermediate must **open towards the tetrahedral sp³ carbon** (which holds both the H and the E). It spans exactly 5 carbons.

((page 20))

**Step 2: Deprotonation (Fast Step)**
*   The intermediate rapidly loses a proton ($H^+$).
*   The two electrons from the C-H bond collapse back into the ring, **restoring the delocalised $\pi$-system** and the aromatic stability.

((page 21))

> [!danger] Examiner Trap
> When drawing the mechanism:
> 1. The curly arrow in Step 1 MUST start *from the circle* (inside the hexagon) and point exactly to the $E^+$.
> 2. The curly arrow in Step 2 MUST start exactly *from the center of the C-H bond* and point *into the ring*.
> 3. Do not draw the positive charge in the intermediate outside the horseshoe. It goes *inside* the broken circle!

# Note 3: 🧪 Core Electrophilic Substitution Reactions of Benzene
**Tags:** #Chemistry/A2 #Organic/Arenes/Reactions

To react with benzene, electrophiles must be incredibly strong. Therefore, a **catalyst (halogen carrier)** or rigorous conditions are always required to generate a powerful electrophile.

### 1. Nitration
*   **Reagents:** Concentrated Nitric Acid ($HNO_3$) and Concentrated Sulfuric Acid ($H_2SO_4$).
*   **Conditions:** Reflux at **25 °C to 60 °C** (Usually exactly $50^\circ C$).
*   **Electrophile:** Nitronium ion ($NO_2^+$).
*   *Note:* If temp > $60^\circ C$, multiple substitutions occur (e.g., 1,3-dinitrobenzene).

> [!important] Generating the Electrophile
> $H_2SO_4$ is a stronger acid than $HNO_3$, so it forces $HNO_3$ to act as a *base* and accept a proton!
> $HNO_3 + 2H_2SO_4 \rightarrow NO_2^+ + 2HSO_4^- + H_3O^+$ ((page 35))

### 2. Halogenation (Chlorination/Bromination)
*   **Reagents:** Dry $Cl_2$ or $Br_2(l)$.
*   **Conditions:** Room temperature, in the dark, presence of a halogen carrier catalyst (e.g., anhydrous $AlCl_3$, $AlBr_3$, $FeCl_3$, or $FeBr_3$).
*   **Electrophile:** Halonium ion ($Cl^+$ or $Br^+$).

> [!example] Catalyst Equations for Bromination ((page 26))
> **Generation:** $Br_2 + FeBr_3 \rightarrow Br^+ + [FeBr_4]^-$
> **Regeneration:** $[FeBr_4]^- + H^+ \rightarrow HBr + FeBr_3$

### 3. Friedel-Crafts Alkylation
Adds an alkyl group ($-R$) to the ring.
*   **Reagents:** Halogenoalkane (e.g., $CH_3Cl$) and anhydrous $AlCl_3$.
*   **Conditions:** Heat under reflux.
*   **Electrophile:** Carbocation ($CH_3^+$).
*   *Warning:* The product (alkylbenzene) is *more reactive* than benzene, often leading to unwanted polyalkylation. ((page 28))

### 4. Friedel-Crafts Acylation
Adds an acyl group ($-COR$) to the ring.
*   **Reagents:** Acyl chloride (e.g., $CH_3COCl$) and anhydrous $AlCl_3$.
*   **Conditions:** Heat under reflux.
*   **Electrophile:** Acylium ion ($CH_3C^+==O$).
*   *Advantage:* The product (a ketone) is *deactivated*, preventing multiple substitutions! ((page 31))

# Note 4: 🔄 Reactions of Alkylbenzenes (Methylbenzene)
**Tags:** #Chemistry/A2 #Organic/Arenes/Alkylbenzenes

Adding an alkyl group to a benzene ring drastically changes its reactivity profile. The methyl group exerts a **positive inductive effect (+I)**, pushing electron density into the ring.

### 1. Ring vs. Side-Chain Halogenation
The conditions entirely dictate where the halogen goes! This is a classic exam discriminator.

**Scenario A: Substitution in the Ring (Electrophilic Substitution)**
*   **Reagents:** $Cl_2(g)$ + $AlCl_3$ catalyst.
*   **Conditions:** Room temperature, in the dark.
*   **Products:** A mixture of 2-chloromethylbenzene and 4-chloromethylbenzene (because $-CH_3$ is 2,4-directing).
*   ((page 43))

**Scenario B: Substitution on the Side-Chain (Free Radical Substitution)**
*   **Reagents:** $Cl_2(g)$.
*   **Conditions:** UV light / boiling.
*   **Products:** (Chloromethyl)benzene ($C_6H_5CH_2Cl$) + $HCl$.
*   ((page 44))

> [!tip] Visualizing the difference
> UV light = Alkane rules (Free Radical Substitution on the side chain).
> Catalyst = Arene rules (Electrophilic Substitution on the ring).

### 2. Complete Oxidation of the Side-Chain
Regardless of how long the alkyl chain is (methyl, ethyl, propyl), rigorous oxidation will snap the C-C bonds and leave only one carbon attached to the ring, fully oxidising it to a carboxyl group.

*   **Reagents:** Hot alkaline $KMnO_4$, followed by acidification with dilute acid (e.g., dilute $H_2SO_4$).
*   **Observation:** Purple $MnO_4^-$ turns to a brown precipitate of $MnO_2$ (in alkali), which clears upon acidification to yield a white precipitate of Benzoic acid.
*   **Product:** Benzoic acid ($C_6H_5COOH$).
*   **Crucial Condition:** The carbon directly attached to the benzene ring *must* have at least one C-H bond for this to work. A tertiary butyl group ($-C(CH_3)_3$) will **not** oxidise!
*   ((page 47))

# Note 5: 🧭 Substituent Effects: Directing Groups & Reactivity
**Tags:** #Chemistry/A2 #Organic/Arenes/DirectingGroups

When a substituent is already on the benzene ring, it dictates two things for the *next* incoming electrophile: **Rate of reaction** and **Position of substitution**. ((page 51))

### 1. Activating Groups (2,4-Directing)
Groups that **donate** electrons into the ring increase the electron density, making the ring *more attractive* to electrophiles. The reaction is **faster** than with plain benzene.
*   **Mechanism of donation:** Either via positive inductive effect (alkyl) or via overlap of a lone pair with the $\pi$-ring (p-orbital resonance).
*   **Positions:** They direct the incoming electrophile to the **ortho (2)** and **para (4)** positions.
*   **Syllabus Examples:** 
    *   **$-OH$ (Phenol):** Overlap of Oxygen's lone pair with the $\pi$-system heavily activates the ring. Reacts with *dilute* $HNO_3$ (no $H_2SO_4$ needed!) and decolourises $Br_2(aq)$ at room temp. ((page 63))
    *   **$-NH_2$ (Phenylamine):** Nitrogen lone pair heavily activates the ring.
    *   **$-R$ (Alkyl/Methyl):** Positive inductive effect (+I). ((page 40))

> [!info] Why 2,4? 
> Electron donation specifically stabilizes the carbocation intermediate when the electrophile attacks at positions 2 and 4, by localizing positive charge adjacent to the electron-donating group. ((page 58))

### 2. Deactivating Groups (3-Directing)
Groups that **withdraw** electrons from the ring (via a heavily $\delta+$ atom directly attached to the ring) decrease the electron density. The reaction is **slower** than plain benzene.
*   **Positions:** They heavily destabilize attack at positions 2 and 4, making the **meta (3)** position the "least bad" pathway.
*   **Syllabus Examples:** 
    *   **$-NO_2$ (Nitrobenzene)**
    *   **$-COOH$ (Benzoic acid)**
    *   **$-COR$ (Ketones/Aldehydes)**
    *   ((page 55))

### 3. The Exception: Halogens (Deactivating but 2,4-Directing)
*   **Halogens ($-Cl, -Br$):** Because halogens are highly electronegative, they withdraw electron density inductively (-I effect), making the ring **less reactive (deactivated)** than benzene.
*   However, they possess lone pairs that can still undergo resonance donation (+M effect) to selectively stabilize the intermediate at the 2 and 4 positions. Therefore, they are **2,4-directors**. ((page 69))

> [!check] Top-in-World Summary Table
> | Substituent already on ring | Reactivity vs Benzene | Directs to Position... |
> | :--- | :--- | :--- |
> | **$-OH$, $-NH_2$** | Much Faster (Activated) | 2, 4 (and 6) |
> | **$-R$ (Alkyl)** | Faster (Activated) | 2, 4 |
> | **$-Cl, -Br$** | **Slower** (Deactivated) | **2, 4** *(The Trap!)* |
> | **$-NO_2$, $-COOH$, $-COR$** | Slower (Deactivated) | 3 |

# Note 6: 🛢️ Destruction of the Ring: Hydrogenation
**Tags:** #Chemistry/A2 #Organic/Arenes/Addition

Although benzene strongly resists addition reactions, its aromaticity *can* be forcefully broken if overwhelming energy is applied.

*   **Reaction:** Complete Hydrogenation.
*   **Reagents:** Hydrogen gas ($H_2$).
*   **Conditions:** **Finely divided Nickel (Raney Nickel) or Platinum catalyst**, high temperature (**$200^\circ C$**), and elevated pressure.
*   **Product:** Cyclohexane ($C_6H_{12}$).
*   **Equation:** $C_6H_6 + 3H_2 \xrightarrow{Ni, 200^\circ C} C_6H_{12}$
*   **Significance:** This reaction proves that benzene does indeed contain the equivalent of 3 double bonds stoichiometrically, but the harsh conditions required prove how stable the delocalised $\pi$-system is compared to normal alkenes (which hydrogenate at much lower temperatures). ((page 37))
