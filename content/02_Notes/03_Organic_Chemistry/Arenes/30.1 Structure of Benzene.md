## 1. The Carbon Skeleton
In a benzene ring, each carbon atom bonds to only **three other atoms** (two neighbouring carbons and one hydrogen) via **3 sigma bonds**.

![‎|300](b0afa.svg)




>[!setup] Hybridisation 
>- The carbon atom mixes one $s$-orbital and two $p$-orbitals to create three identical $sp^2$ hybrid orbitals.
>- One $p$-orbital remains unhybridised
>
>![‎|300](https://chem.libretexts.org/@api/deki/files/267508/clipboard_ef3d10ef03215f092a4acf530bb9a5c50.png?revision=1)

>[!setup] Bonding
>- The 3 $sp^2$ orbitals allow each carbon to form 3 sigma bonds (two neighbouring carbons and one hydrogen).



>[!setup] VSEPR 
>- To keep the negatively charged electron clouds as far apart as possible, these orbitals arrange themselves in a flat, **trigonal planar** shape.
>- The angle between these $\sigma$ bonds is exactly $120$°



## 2. Delocalised $\pi$-Electron Cloud
>[!setup] Merging of $p$-orbitals
>- The six unhybridised $p$-orbitals, one on each carbon atom, are all parallel to each other and perpendicular to the plane of the carbon ring.
>
>![‎|400](bc009.svg)
>
>- Each $p$-orbital overlaps sideways equally with adjacent $p$-orbitals, forming a delocalised, six-centre molecular $\pi$-bond above and below the plane of the carbon ring.
>  
>  ![‎|400](8edce.svg)


## 3. The Resulting Structure
Combining the carbon ring and delocalised electron clouds gives benzene's signature structure 


![‎](fuck.png)
>[!important] Key Features of Benzene's Structure
>- Each carbon atom has a bond angle of 120° with adjacent atoms and a trigonal planar shape.
>- All carbon-carbon bonds are equal in length and strength.
>- All carbon-hydrogen bonds are equal in length and strength.
>- Unhybridised $p$-orbitals overlap sideways.
>- Delocalised $\pi$-electron system
