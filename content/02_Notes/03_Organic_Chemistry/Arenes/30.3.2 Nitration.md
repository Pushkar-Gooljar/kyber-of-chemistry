## 2. Mechanism: $S_{E}Ar$


>[!mechanism] Nitration: Electrophilic Substitution 
>![](0021e.svg)
>- The nitronium ion is attracted to the delocalised $\pi$ bond of benzene. 
>- It eventually breaks the ring of electrons and forms a dative $\sigma$ bond to one of the carbon atoms of the ring.
>- The remaining four $\pi$ electrons are spread over the remaining five carbon atoms of the ring, in a five-centre delocalised orbital, forming an intermediate carbocation.
>- The carbocation loses a proton, the electrons of the former $\ce{C-H}$ bond reform the sextet of $\pi$ electrons.

## 3. Formation of Electrophile
>[!result] Formation of $\ce{NO_{2}+}$
>$$\large\ce{HNO_{3} + 2H_{2}SO_{4} -> NO_{2}+ + 2HSO_{4}- + H_{3}O+}$$


>[!mechanism] Step 1: Protonation of Nitric Acid
>$$\large\ce{\underset{Base}{HNO_{3}} + \underset{Acid}{H_{2}SO_{4}}-> H_{2}O+-NO_{2} + HSO_{4}-}$$
>- $\ce{HNO_{3}}$ is a strong acid, but $\ce{H_{2}SO_{4}}$ is even stronger.
>- The $\ce{H_{2}SO_{4}}$ acts as proton donor while the $\ce{HNO_{3}}$ is forced to act as proton acceptor.
>- Nitric acid has a hydroxyl group (-OH) attached to its central nitrogen. The oxygen atom in this -OH group has lone pairs of electrons.
>- One of the lone pairs forms a dative bond with the $\ce{H+}$ donated by $\ce{H_{2}SO_{4}}$.
>
>This forms **protonated nitric acid** (an intermediate) and leaves behind hydrogen 
>sulfate ion ($\ce{HSO_{4}-}$).

>[!mechanism] Step 2: Formation of Nitronium Ion ($\ce{NO_{2}+}$)
>$$\large\ce{H_{2}O+-NO_{2} -> NO_{2}+ + H_{2}O}$$
>
>- The protonated nitric acid is highly unstable because of the positive charge on an oxygen atom.
>- The bond between oxygen and nitrogen breaks, and the electrons go entirely to the oxygen.
>
>An $\ce{H_{2}O}$ molecule breaks away, leaving behind a highly reactive **Nitronium Ion ($\ce{NO_{2}+}$)**.

>[!mechanism] Step 3: Protonation of the byproduct water
>$$\large\ce{H_{2}O + H_{2}SO_{4} -> H_{3}O+ + HSO_{4}-}$$
>- The reaction is taking place in an extremely acidic environment.
>- The neutral water molecule produced in step 2 cannot exist for long in this environment.
>- It immediately acts as a base and grabs a proton form a *second* molecule of sulfuric acid.
>
>This forms a hydronium ion ($\ce{H_{3}O+}$) and a second hydrogen sulfate ion ($\ce{HSO_{4}-}$).
>
>
>
>>[!tip]- Importance of this step
>>- In reality, the reactions of all 3 steps are reversible.
>>- According to **Le Chatelier's Principle:**
>>	- By constantly turning the byproduct water into hydronium, the water is removed from the equilibrium of **Step 2**.
>>	- This prevents the reaction from going backwards and drives the entire process forward, ensuring a large, steady supply of $\ce{NO_{2}+}$ electrophile for the benzene to react with.


## 4. Regeneration of Catalyst
>[!mechanism] Regeneration of Catalyst
>$$\large\ce{HSO_{4}- + H+ -> H_{2}SO_{4}}$$
>- $\ce{H+}$ produced by deprotonation of benzene combines with hydrogen sulfate ion to form sulfuric acid.

>[!tip] Catching Point: Dilution
>- While the $\ce{H_{2}SO_{4}}$ is completely regenerated chemically, there is a practical issue in the lab.
>- Generation of electrophile produces water ($\ce{H_{2}O}/$/$\ce{H_{3}O+}$) as byproduct.
>- As the reaction runs, the water begins to dilute the concentrated sulfuric acid.
>- Eventually, the acid mixture becomes too watered down to effectively protonate the nitric acid, the reaction slows to a halt.
