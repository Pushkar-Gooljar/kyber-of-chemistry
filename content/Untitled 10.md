>[!warning] Dropwise $\ce{OH-}$ or $\ce{NH3}$ Addition **(NOT Ligand Exchange!)**
>**Reagent:** $\ce{NaOH(aq)}$ or $\ce{NH3(aq)}$ added dropwise.
>
>**Observations:** **pale blue precipitate** (insoluble in excess $\ce{NaOH}$).
>
>**Complex Formed:** $\ce{[Cu(H2O)4(OH)2](s)}$ (Neutral and insoluble)
>
>**Type of Reaction:** Acid-Base / Deprotonation
>
>**Reactions:**
>* **With $\ce{OH-}$:**
>$$\ce{\underbrace{ [Cu(H2O)6]^{2+}(aq) }_{ \text{Blue Solution} } + 2OH^-(aq) -> \underbrace{ [Cu(H2O)4(OH)2](s) }_{ \text{Pale Blue Ppt} } + 2H2O(l)}$$
>* **With $\ce{NH3}$:**
>$$\ce{\underbrace{ [Cu(H2O)6]^{2+}(aq) }_{ \text{Blue Solution} } + 2NH3(aq) -> \underbrace{ [Cu(H2O)4(OH)2](s) }_{ \text{Pale Blue Ppt} } + 2NH4+(aq)}$$
>
>**Explanation:**
>- **No ligand exchange occurs here.** No incoming ligand enters the coordination sphere to replace an existing one.
>- **Role of $\ce{Cu^{2+}}$:** The high charge density of the central $\ce{Cu^{2+}}$ ion polarizes the $\ce{O-H}$ bonds in the water ligands, making the hydrogen atoms highly acidic.
>- **Acid-Base Mechanism:**
>  - $\ce{[Cu(H2O)6]^{2+}}$ acts as a **Brønsted-Lowry acid** (proton donor).
>  - $\ce{OH-}$ (or $\ce{NH3}$) acts as a **Brønsted-Lowry base** (proton acceptor).
>  - The base removes $\ce{H+}$ protons directly from two of the water ligands.
>
>**Crucial Concepts for Exams:**
>- **Origin of the $\ce{OH-}$ ligands:** The $\ce{OH-}$ ligands inside the final precipitate are the *original water ligands* that lost a proton, not the free $\ce{OH-}$ ions added from the burette/pipette.
>- **Origin of the produced $\ce{H2O}$ / $\ce{NH4+}$:** These are formed when the added base ($\ce{OH-}$ or $\ce{NH3}$) accepts the proton ($\ce{H+}$) removed from the complex.
