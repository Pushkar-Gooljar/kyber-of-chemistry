## Zinc Carbonate (white powder)
$$
\large
\ce{ZnCO_{3}(s) -> ZnO(s) + CO_{2}(g)} 
$$
>[!warning]
>For laboratory use, basic zinc carbonate is used most of the times which decomposes via the equation:
>$$\Large \ce{Zn_5(CO_3)_2(OH)_6 -> 5ZnO + 2CO_2 + 3H_2O}$$

>[!example] Observations
>- Condensation is observed on cool parts of test tube. *(NOTE: because of the basic zinc carbonate)*
>- A yellow solid is formed from the white solid.
>- On cooling the yellow residue turns white.
>- A gas is liberated which gives a white precipitate when bubbled through limewater.
>- The gas is carbon dioxide.

>[!tip]
>- Hot zinc oxide is yellow and cool zinc oxide is white.
## Sodium Nitrate (white solid)
$$
\Large
\ce{2NaNO_{3}(s) -> 2NaNO_{2}(s) + O_{2}(g)}
$$
>[!warning]
>Nitrogen dioxide is **NOT** formed.

>[!example] Observations
>- The white solid melts.
>- Effervescence is observed.
>- A gas is is liberated which re-lights a glowing splint.
>- The gas is oxygen.
>- **On cooling** a pale yellow solid is formed.

>[!tip] Explanation
>- Sodium nitrate has a melting point of about $300^{\circ}\text{C}$, a Bunsen burner flame can reach above this temperature so the solid melts.
>- Thermal decomposition of sodium nitrate produces oxygen which escapes the melted solid as bubbles.
>- The oxygen relights the glowing splint.
>- On cooling the melted solid solidifies into solid sodium nitrite which is naturally pale yellow.

## Ammonium Carbonate (white solid)
$$
\Large
\ce{(NH_{4})2CO_{3}(s)-> 2NH_{3}(g) + CO_{2}(g) + H_{2}O(g)}
$$
>[!warning]
>All products are gasses so no residue is left.

>[!example] Observations
>- Initially a white solid.
>- Water vapour is observed.
>- Condensation is seen on cooler parts of test tube.
>- Solid disappears.
>- A pungent smelling gas which turns damp red litmus paper blue is liberated.
>- The gas is ammonia.
>- A gas is liberated which gives a white precipitate when bubbled through limewater.
>- The gas is carbon dioxide.

## Ammonium Sulfate (white solid)
$$
\Large
\ce{(NH4)2SO4(s) ->[gentle heat] NH4HSO4(l) + NH3(g)}
$$
$$
\Large
\ce{NH4HSO4(l) ->[strong heat] NH3(g) + H2O(g) + SO3(g)}
$$
>[!example] Observations
>- Initially white solid.
>- Solid melts.
>- Steam is produced.
>- White smoke is observed and sublimation occurs on walls of test tube.
>- A gas is liberated which turns damp red litmus paper turns blue.
>- No residue.
>- A gas is liberated which turns damp blue litmus paper red.

>[!tip] Explanation
>- On gentle heating $\ce{(NH_{4})2SO_{4}}$ forms $\ce{NH_{4}HSO_{4}}$ which is a liquid, and ammonia gas.
>- On strong heating, the $\ce{NH_{4}HSO_{4}}$ decomposes into ammonia, water, and sulfur trioxide.
>- Ammonia has a lower $M_{r}$ than sulfur trioxide, it therefore reaches the top faster and turns damp red litmus paper blue.
>- After some time sulfur trioxide reaches the top and turns damp blue litmus paper red as it is an acidic gas.
>- The ammonia reacts with acidic gasses to reform a solid ammonium salt hence the sublimation.
>- Since all products are gasses no residue is left.

## Basic Copper (II) Carbonate (blue-green powder)
$$
\Large
\ce{CuCO3.Cu(OH)2(s) -> 2CuO(s) + CO2(g) + H2O(g)}
$$
>[!example] Observations
>- Initially a blue-green powder
>- Steam is produced.
>- Solid fluidises.
>- A black residue is formed.
>- A gas is liberated which gives a white precipitate when bubbled through limewater.
>- The gas is carbon dioxide.

>[!tip] Explanation
>- The production of steam and carbon dioxide push the fine powder particles into the air.
>- This causes the dry powder to hop around violently as if it was a boiling liquid.
>- This process is known as fluidisation.

## Sodium Carbonate + Sodium Hydrogensulfate (white solid)
$$
\Large
\ce{2NaHSO4(s/l) + Na2CO3(s) -> 2Na2SO4(s) + H2O(g) + CO2(g)}
$$

>[!example] Observations
>- Condensation is seen on the cooler parts of the test tube.
>- A gas is liberated which gives a white precipitate when bubbled through limewater.
>- The gas is carbon dioxide.
>- White residue is observed on cooling.

>[!tip] Explanation
>- Sodium carbonate is a basic salt.
>- Sodium hydrogensulfate is an acidic salt.
>- Sodium hydrogensulfate has a low melting point.
>- When the mixture is heated, it melts allowing the acidic hydrogen ions to contact and react vigorously with the carbonate.
>- The acid-carbonate neutralisation produces water and carbon dioxide.

## Potassium Manganate (VII) (dark purple crystals)

### Heat
$$
\Large
\ce{2KMnO_{4}(s)->K_{2}MnO_{2}(s) + MnO_{2}(s) + O_{2}(g)}
$$
>[!example] Observations
>- Initially dark purple crystals.
>- Solid jumps around in test tube.
>- A gas is liberated which relights a glowing splint.
>- The gas is oxygen.
>- A black residue is formed.

### Add distilled water to residue from heating
$$
\Large
\ce{3MnO4^2- (green) + 2H2O -> 2MnO4^- (purple) + MnO2 (black solid) + 4OH^-}
$$

>[!example] Observations
>- A purple solution is formed.

>[!tip] Explanation
>- The $\ce{MnO4^2-}$ ion undergoes a disproportionation reaction forming $\ce{MnO4^-}$ which gives the purple colour to the solution.
>- This happens because the $\ce{MnO4^2-}$ ion is unstable in neutral water.

### Add aqueous sodium hydroxide to the residue from heating
>[!example] Observations
>- A green solution is formed.

>[!tip] Explanation
>- No disproportionation reaction occurs as $\ce{MnO4^2-}$ is stable in alkaline conditions.
>- The $\ce{MnO4^2-}$ gives the solution its green colour.


### Add the residue to alkaline potassium manganate (vii) and filter
$$
\Large
\ce{2MnO4^- (purple) + MnO2 (black) + 4OH^- -> 3MnO4^2- (green) + 2H2O}
$$
>[!example] Observations
>- Filtrate is a dark green solution

>[!tip] Explanation
>- The reaction produces $\ce{MnO_{4}^2-}$ which is green and stable **only** in alkaline environments.

#### Add dilute sulfuric acid to the filtrate
$$
\Large
\ce{3MnO4^2- (green) + 4H^+ -> 2MnO4^- (purple) + MnO2 (brown/black ppt) + 2H2O}
$$
>[!example] Observations
>- Solution turns purple

>[!tip] Explanation
>- Since the $\ce{MnO_{4}^2-}$ is only stable in alkaline environments, adding acid destroys it. It undergoes a disproportionation reaction and forms $\ce{MnO_{4}-}$ which is purple.
