# 🧪 Masterclass: Transition Metal Reactions & Observations

> [!abstract] Reaction Types
> When a reagent is added to an aqueous transition metal ion, one of three things happens:
> 1. **Acid-Base / Precipitation / Deprotonation:** The ligand (usually water) loses a proton ($H^+$) to the added base ($OH^-$ or $NH_3$). A solid neutral complex forms.
> 2. **Ligand Exchange / Substitution:** One ligand is completely replaced by another (e.g., $H_2O$ replaced by $NH_3$, $Cl^-$, or $EDTA^{4-}$).
> 3. **Redox:** The transition metal changes its oxidation state.

---

## 1. Copper Chemistry ($Cu^{2+}$)
**Starting Solution:** Aqueous Copper(II) Sulfate contains $[Cu(H_2O)_6]^{2+}$ (Pale blue solution).

> [!example] 1. Addition of limited $NaOH(aq)$ or $NH_3(aq)$
> **Type of Reaction:** Acid-base / Precipitation / Deprotonation
> **Observation:** Pale blue solution $\rightarrow$ **Pale blue precipitate**
> **Equation:** 
> $$[Cu(H_2O)_6]^{2+}_{(aq)} + 2OH^-_{(aq)} \rightarrow Cu(OH)_2(H_2O)_{4(s)} + 2H_2O_{(l)}$$
> *(Note: The MS also accepts $[Cu(H_2O)_6]^{2+} + 2OH^- \rightarrow Cu(OH)_{2(s)} + 6H_2O$)*

> [!example] 2. Addition of excess $NH_3(aq)$
> **Type of Reaction:** Ligand Exchange / Substitution
> **Observation:** Pale blue precipitate dissolves $\rightarrow$ **Deep / dark / royal blue solution**
> **Equation:** 
> $$Cu(OH)_2(H_2O)_{4(s)} + 4NH_3 \rightarrow [Cu(NH_3)_4(H_2O)_2]^{2+}_{(aq)} + 2H_2O + 2OH^-$$
> *(Catch Point: This is a **partial** ligand substitution. Only 4 waters are replaced by ammonia. The shape remains octahedral).*

> [!example] 3. Addition of excess concentrated $HCl(aq)$
> **Type of Reaction:** Ligand Exchange (and change of geometry)
> **Observation:** Pale blue solution $\rightarrow$ **Yellow solution**
> **Equation:** 
> $$[Cu(H_2O)_6]^{2+}_{(aq)} + 4Cl^-_{(aq)} \rightleftharpoons [CuCl_4]^{2-}_{(aq)} + 6H_2O_{(l)}$$
> *(Geometry changes from **Octahedral** to **Tetrahedral** due to large $Cl^-$ ligands repelling each other).*

> [!warning] 4. Reaction with Iodide Ions ($I^-$)
> **Type of Reaction:** Redox / Disproportionation of Iodine
> **Observation:** Pale blue solution $\rightarrow$ **White precipitate in a brown solution**
> **Equation:** 
> $$2Cu^{2+}_{(aq)} + 4I^-_{(aq)} \rightarrow 2CuI_{(s)} + I_{2(aq)}$$
> *(MS Tip: The white ppt is Copper(I) Iodide, the brown solution is aqueous Iodine).*

## 2. Cobalt Chemistry ($Co^{2+}$)
**Starting Solution:** Aqueous Cobalt(II) salts contain $[Co(H_2O)_6]^{2+}$ (Pink solution).

> [!example] 1. Addition of limited $NaOH(aq)$ or $NH_3(aq)$
> **Type of Reaction:** Precipitation / Acid-base
> **Observation:** Pink solution $\rightarrow$ **Blue precipitate**
> **Equation:** 
> $$[Co(H_2O)_6]^{2+}_{(aq)} + 2OH^-_{(aq)} \rightarrow Co(OH)_2(H_2O)_{4(s)} + 2H_2O_{(l)}$$

> [!example] 2. Addition of excess $NH_3(aq)$
> **Type of Reaction:** Ligand Exchange
> **Observation:** Blue precipitate dissolves $\rightarrow$ **Brown / Yellow-brown solution**
> **Equation:** 
> $$Co(OH)_2(H_2O)_{4(s)} + 6NH_3 \rightarrow [Co(NH_3)_6]^{2+}_{(aq)} + 2H_2O + 2OH^-$$
> *(Note: The $Co^{2+}$ complex is readily oxidised by air to $[Co(NH_3)_6]^{3+}$ which is a darker brown/red colour).*

> [!example] 3. Addition of excess concentrated $HCl(aq)$
> **Type of Reaction:** Ligand Exchange
> **Observation:** Pink solution $\rightarrow$ **Blue solution**
> **Equation:** 
> $$[Co(H_2O)_6]^{2+}_{(aq)} + 4Cl^-_{(aq)} \rightleftharpoons [CoCl_4]^{2-}_{(aq)} + 6H_2O_{(l)}$$
> *(Geometry changes from **Octahedral** to **Tetrahedral**).*

## 3. Iron Chemistry ($Fe^{2+}$ and $Fe^{3+}$)

### Iron(II) - $[Fe(H_2O)_6]^{2+}$
**Starting Solution:** Pale green solution.

> [!example] 1. Addition of $NaOH(aq)$ or $NH_3(aq)$
> **Type of Reaction:** Precipitation
> **Observation:** Pale green solution $\rightarrow$ **Green precipitate**
> **Equation:** $[Fe(H_2O)_6]^{2+} + 2OH^- \rightarrow Fe(OH)_{2(s)} + 6H_2O$
> *The precipitate turns **brown on standing in air** due to oxidation to $Fe(OH)_3$.*

### Iron(III) - $[Fe(H_2O)_6]^{3+}$
**Starting Solution:** Yellow / pale brown / orange solution.

> [!example] 2. Addition of $NaOH(aq)$ or $NH_3(aq)$
> **Type of Reaction:** Precipitation
> **Observation:** Yellow solution $\rightarrow$ **Red-brown / Brown precipitate**
> **Equation:** $[Fe(H_2O)_6]^{3+} + 3OH^- \rightarrow Fe(OH)_{3(s)} + 6H_2O$

> [!example] 3. Addition of Thiocyanate ions ($SCN^-$) [NEW Syllabus Focus]
> **Type of Reaction:** Ligand Exchange
> **Observation:** Yellow solution $\rightarrow$ **Blood-red solution**
> **Equation:** $[Fe(H_2O)_6]^{3+} + SCN^- \rightleftharpoons [Fe(H_2O)_5(SCN)]^{2+} + H_2O$
> *(Often used as a sensitive test for $Fe^{3+}$ ions).*

## 4. Polydentate Ligand Exchanges (The Chelate Effect)

Replacing monodentate ligands with bidentate (e.g., $en$, $C_2O_4^{2-}$) or hexadentate (e.g., $EDTA^{4-}$) ligands leads to highly stable complexes.

> [!info] The Chelate Effect (Thermodynamics)
> Ligand exchanges involving polydentate ligands cause a significant **increase in the number of particles** in solution (e.g., 2 particles $\rightarrow$ 7 particles).
> *   This causes a large increase in **entropy** ($\Delta S > 0$).
> *   Because $\Delta G = \Delta H - T\Delta S$, the large positive $\Delta S$ makes $\Delta G$ very negative.
> *   Therefore, the reaction is highly feasible and the complex formed is exceptionally stable (huge $K_{stab}$).

> [!example] Copper(II) with $EDTA^{4-}$
> **Equation:** $[Cu(H_2O)_6]^{2+} + EDTA^{4-} \rightleftharpoons [Cu(EDTA)]^{2-} + 6H_2O$
> **Observation:** Pale blue solution $\rightarrow$ **Deep blue solution**
> *(Geometry remains octahedral, coordination number remains 6).*

> [!example] Iron(III) with Ethanedioate ($C_2O_4^{2-}$)
> **Equation:** $[Fe(H_2O)_6]^{3+} + 3C_2O_4^{2-} \rightleftharpoons [Fe(C_2O_4)_3]^{3-} + 6H_2O$
> **Observation:** Yellow solution $\rightarrow$ **Green solution**
> *(Frequently tested to draw optical isomers of this complex).*

## 5. Silver Chemistry ($Ag^+$)

Silver forms linear complexes with a coordination number of 2.

> [!example] 1. Formation of Tollens' Reagent (Diamminesilver(I) ion)
> **Step 1:** Add $NaOH$ to $AgNO_3$ to form a **brown precipitate** of Silver(I) Oxide.
> $$2Ag^+ + 2OH^- \rightarrow Ag_2O_{(s)} + H_2O$$
> **Step 2:** Add excess $NH_3(aq)$ to dissolve the ppt and form a **colourless solution**.
> $$Ag_2O_{(s)} + 4NH_3 + H_2O \rightarrow 2[Ag(NH_3)_2]^+ + 2OH^-$$

> [!example] 2. Dissolving Silver Halides
> **Equation:** $AgCl_{(s)} + 2NH_3 \rightarrow [Ag(NH_3)_2]^+_{(aq)} + Cl^-_{(aq)}$
> *(Also works with concentrated $NH_3$ and $AgBr$).*

## 6. Key Transition Metal Redox Reactions

You must be able to use $E^\theta$ values from the Data Booklet to prove these reactions happen, but memorising the core equations saves massive amounts of time.

> [!example] 1. Iron(II) and Manganate(VII) Titrations
> **Conditions:** Acidified (usually dilute $H_2SO_4$)
> **Equation:** $MnO_4^- + 8H^+ + 5Fe^{2+} \rightarrow Mn^{2+} + 5Fe^{3+} + 4H_2O$
> **Colour Change at End-Point:** Colourless/pale green $\rightarrow$ **Pale pink** (first permanent pink colour marks the end-point).

> [!example] 2. Iron(II) and Dichromate(VI) Titrations
> **Conditions:** Acidified
> **Equation:** $Cr_2O_7^{2-} + 14H^+ + 6Fe^{2+} \rightarrow 2Cr^{3+} + 6Fe^{3+} + 7H_2O$
> **Colour Change:** Orange $\rightarrow$ **Green**

> [!example] 3. Homogeneous Catalysis by $Fe^{2+}/Fe^{3+}$ (Persulfate & Iodide)
> The reaction between $S_2O_8^{2-}$ and $I^-$ is slow because both are negatively charged (repulsion). $Fe^{2+}$ or $Fe^{3+}$ acts as a homogeneous catalyst.
> **Step 1:** $2Fe^{3+} + 2I^- \rightarrow 2Fe^{2+} + I_2$
> **Step 2:** $2Fe^{2+} + S_2O_8^{2-} \rightarrow 2Fe^{3+} + 2SO_4^{2-}$

> [!example] 4. Vanadium Oxidation States
> Vanadium can be reduced from +5 to +2 using Zinc in acidic conditions.
> *   $VO_2^+$ (Oxidation state +5) $\rightarrow$ **Yellow**
> *   $VO^{2+}$ (Oxidation state +4) $\rightarrow$ **Blue**
> *   $V^{3+}$ (Oxidation state +3) $\rightarrow$ **Green**
> *   $V^{2+}$ (Oxidation state +2) $\rightarrow$ **Violet / Purple**
> *(Mnemonic: **Y**ou **B**etter **G**et **V**anadium).*
