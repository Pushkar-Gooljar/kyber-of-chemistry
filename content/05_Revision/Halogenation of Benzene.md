What are the reagents and conditions required for the **bromination** of benzene?
- **Reagents:** Benzene + non-aqueous bromine ($Br_{2}$)
- **Catalyst:** Anhydrous iron (III) bromide ($\ce{FeBr_{3}}$) 
- **Conditions:** Warm

What are the reagents and conditions required for the **chlorination** of benzene?
- **Reagents:** Benzene + chlorine gas ($Cl_{2}$)
- **Catalyst:** Anhydrous aluminium chloride ($\ce{AlCl_{3}}$) or iron (III) chloride ($\ce{FeCl_{3}}$)
- **Conditions:** Room temperature

Why does benzene require a **halogen carrier catalyst** (like $\ce{AlCl_{3}}$) to react with halogens?
- Benzene has a highly stable delocalised $\pi$ electron ring.
- The non-polar halogen molecule is not strong enough to disrupt this ring on its own.
- The catalyst is needed to generate a powerful electrophile

Explain the steps in the bromination of benzene
![[Pasted image 20260310204708.png]]
- The bromine cation is attracted to the delocalised $\pi$ system of benzene.
- It eventually breaks the ring of electrons and forms a dative $\sigma$ bond to one of the carbon atoms of the ring.
- The remaining four $\pi$ electrons are spread over the remaining five carbon atoms of the ring, in a five-centre delocalised orbital, forming an intermediate carbocation.
- The carbocation loses a proton, the electrons of the former $\ce{C-H}$ bond reform the sextet of $\pi$ electrons
