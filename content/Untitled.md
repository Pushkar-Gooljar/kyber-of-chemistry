### 1. General Properties & Definitions

>[!question] 9701_w25_qp_44_qu_001
>**Define a transition element.**
>>[!success]- Answer
>>- Forms (one or more) stable ions / compounds / oxidation states 
>>- With incomplete / partially filled (3)d orbital(s) / d shell / d sub-shell

>[!question] 9701_w25_qp_43_qu_002
>**Explain why transition elements have variable oxidation states.**
>>[!success]- Answer
>>- The (3)d and (4)s sub-shells / orbitals / electrons are close / similar in energy

>[!question] 9701_w25_qp_43_qu_005
>**Explain why transition elements behave as catalysts.**
>>[!success]- Answer
>>- They have more than one (stable) oxidation state / exist in variable oxidation states
>>- Vacant / empty (d) orbitals are energetically accessible
>>- *OR* vacant / empty (d) orbitals can form dative bonds with ligands

>[!question] 9701_w24_qp_43_qu_003
>**Describe the difference between a heterogeneous and a homogeneous catalyst.**
>>[!success]- Answer
>>- (Heterogeneous) catalyst and reactants are in different states / phases
>>- (Homogeneous) catalyst and reactants are in the same states / phases

>[!question] 9701_s22_qp_43_qu_002
>**Explain what is meant by a heterogeneous catalyst. Describe the mode of action of a heterogeneous catalyst in a reaction.**
>>[!success]- Answer
>>- The catalyst and the reactants are in a different state / phase
>>- **Mode of action:**
>>- Adsorption of reactants to the surface of the catalyst
>>- Bonds in the reactants weaken (lowering the activation energy)
>>- Reaction occurs and the products are desorbed

>[!question] 9701_m22_qp_42_qu_001
>**The reaction of $I^-$ ions with persulfate ions, $S_2O_8^{2-}$, can be catalysed by $Fe^{3+}$ ions. Write equations to show how $Fe^{3+}$ catalyses this reaction.**
>>[!success]- Answer
>>- Reduction of $Fe^{3+}$: $2Fe^{3+} + 2I^- \rightarrow 2Fe^{2+} + I_2$
>>- Regeneration of $Fe^{3+}$: $2Fe^{2+} + S_2O_8^{2-} \rightarrow 2Fe^{3+} + 2SO_4^{2-}$

### 2. Complexes, Ligands & Geometry

>[!question] 9701_w25_qp_43_qu_002
>**Define complex ion.**
>>[!success]- Answer
>>- Species or ion formed by a central metal atom/ion
>>- AND surrounded by/bonded to (one or more) ligands

>[!question] 9701_s25_qp_44_qu_001
>**Explain what is meant by a bidentate ligand.**
>>[!success]- Answer
>>- A species with / has two lone pairs of electrons
>>- That form dative (covalent) / co-ordinate bond(s) to a (central) transition-element / metal atom / ion

>[!question] 9701_s23_qp_43_qu_001
>**Explain what is meant by coordination number.**
>>[!success]- Answer
>>- The number of co-ordinate bonds being formed by the metal atom / ion

### 3. Stereoisomerism in Complexes

>[!question] 9701_w25_qp_44_qu_001
>**A student draws the *cis* and *trans* isomers of a square planar complex. Deduce which stereoisomer is polar. Explain your answer.**
>>[!success]- Answer
>>- *Cis* isomer is polar (and *trans* is non-polar)
>>- In the *cis* isomer, dipoles do not cancel / it is asymmetric and has polar bonds
>>- *(In the trans isomer, dipoles cancel / partial charges cancel)*

### 4. Colour of Complexes

>[!question] 9701_w25_qp_44_qu_001
>**The 3d orbitals in an isolated gaseous $Cu^{2+}$ ion are degenerate. Define the term degenerate.**
>>[!success]- Answer
>>- (Orbitals) are at the same energy

>[!question] 9701_w25_qp_44_qu_001
>**Explain why aqueous complex ions of transition elements are usually coloured.**
>>[!success]- Answer
>>- d orbital(s) of different energy / d-d splitting occurs / d sub-shell splits
>>- electron(s) promoted / excited
>>- light / wavelength / frequency / photon absorbed AND complementary colour seen

>[!question] 9701_m24_qp_42_qu_003
>**Aqueous solutions of complexes $[Fe(H_2O)_6]^{3+}$ and $[Fe(H_2O)_5SCN]^{2+}$ are different colours. Explain why these complexes are different colours.**
>>[!success]- Answer
>>- (d–d) energy gap / $\Delta E$ is different
>>- Different frequency / wavelength (of light) absorbed / transmitted / reflected

>[!question] 9701_m22_qp_42_qu_003
>**$TiO^{2+}$(aq) is a colourless ion. Suggest why.**
>>[!success]- Answer
>>- Ti is in +4 oxidation state so no d electrons / $d^0$
>>- Cannot absorb photons / light in visible spectrum OR no wavelength / frequency absorbed in visible spectrum

### 5. Ligand Exchange & Stability Constants ($K_{stab}$)

>[!question] 9701_w25_qp_44_qu_001
>**Define the stability constant ($K_{stab}$) of a complex.**
>>[!success]- Answer
>>- Equilibrium constant for the formation of the complex (ion)
>>- In a solvent / water / solution OR from its constituent ions or molecules

>[!question] 9701_s22_qp_42_qu_001
>**Nickel can form complexes with the bidentate ligands *en* and *tn*. Equilibrium 1 forms $[Ni(en)_3]^{2+}$ ($K_{stab} = 6.76 \times 10^{17}$). Equilibrium 2 forms $[Ni(tn)_3]^{2+}$ ($K_{stab} = 1.86 \times 10^{12}$). Describe what the $K_{stab}$ values indicate about the position of equilibrium. Use the $K_{stab}$ values to deduce which complex is more stable.**
>>[!success]- Answer
>>- The equilibria lie to the right / product side OR equilibrium 1 lies more to the right
>>- AND *en* complex is more stable / $[Ni(en)_3]^{2+}$ is more stable (because it has the highest $K_{stab}$)
